# M12d — Paying M12b's debts

**Status: complete** (2026-09-01). This is the milestone-level record above
three item documents — `M12d-D1.md`, `M12d-D2.md`, `M12d-D3.md` — and two
cross-vendor reviews in `docs/reviews/`. It is written in M12.md's register,
which M12b.md inherited: hypothesis, A/B, verdict, **including the
non-results**, and a refuted claim withdrawn in place rather than deleted. It
does not restate the item documents; it says what the milestone set out to do,
what measurement did to that intention, what it declined, and what it still
owes. Read §1 and §7 if you read nothing else; **read §9 if you are the one
starting M13**, because it is the list.

M12b existed to pay M12's debts and closed owing nine of its own
(`M12b.md` §10). This milestone was built to pay the three that were
engineering rather than scheduling, and to state the two that are neither
honestly. **It paid the three and it did not pay the two**, which is the shape
this document has to be read for.

---

## 1. The result, stated first

**Two of this milestone's three engineering items had their premise corrected
by measurement — the same failure mode M12b recorded on three of its four —
and on both of them the correction is the item's largest contribution.**

- **D2 was owed one third of what it was briefed.** The brief said the
  glyph-spacing heuristic and the croscore-boundary fix came from a *reverted*
  branch and had to be re-derived before being built on. They were on `main`,
  landed 2026-08-29 as `ba8662f` and `10905fe`, and never reverted; what made
  them read as absent is that `conformance/scoreboard.json` carries an
  *earlier* timestamp the same day. `bug_601362` already stood at **0.999117**
  when the item opened. The claim is **withdrawn in place** — `d81ac68`, in
  M12b.md §10 item 8 and in PLAN.md's D2 — and D2's real work was the third
  step, the serif bit, plus a second divergence it found on its own: `fontdb`
  names a face by name ID 16 where the oracle's enumerator uses ID 1. Both
  fixes move **0 of 1675 files**. They are paid on the oracle comparison, not
  on a number, and that is the honest size of them.
- **D3 was briefed against a document that does not exist.** The inherited
  digest said `image_en_fqa` was "552 small images, every one a 1-bit mask
  minified 8.33× in both axes". Instrumented at the dispatch it is **301 draws
  of a 2×2 RGB image carrying a non-co-registered `/SMask`**, the 8.33× being
  the *mask's* — which put the whole cost in `render_masked_image`, an arm
  outside both `Phase::Image` and M12 §9.1's render cache, and therefore inside
  the profile's unattributed residue. Correcting the premise is what located
  the cost. Result: cold **679.7 → 158.7 ms (−76.7%)**, page-graph build
  **−83.7%**, warm walk −40.4%, and the `image` class cold geomean **−14.5%**
  on top of P1's −33.4%.
- **D1's premise held, and its non-result is the more durable half.** One
  `BezPath` buffer per patch in place of one per cell — 28 368 of them on a
  single document — nets **−22.7% / −25.2% / −18.5%** on the three shading
  documents' patch figure, with `shading_coons`, the control, unmoved and
  conformance byte-identical on all 202 page hashes of a direct two-binary
  comparison. Four other directions measured to zero, three of them
  *structurally*. The one that matters: **merging adjacent cells is refuted by
  counting** — the longest run of same-coloured neighbours is 2 and usually 1,
  because the split test and the merge test are the same test with opposite
  signs — so **M12 §3.9's SIMD reopening condition stays unmet and now has a
  reason** rather than a deferral.

Two things run through the milestone and belong here rather than in a footnote.

**A cross-vendor review happened for the first time** (§5). Two Grok reviews,
both **MERGEABLE-WITH-NITS**, and the GPU one found three should-fixes that
were real: a device leaked before the check that would have refused it, a
documented `Error::TargetTooLarge` that was never constructed with a 17 GiB
allocation reachable behind it, and wgpu's default uncaptured-error hook
panicking a host application on a driver reset. All three are fixed. The walk
review's NaN finding was also real **and ran the opposite direction from its
own reasoning** — a missed cull rather than a false one, which is the
difference between a review finding and a rendering bug.

**Measurement integrity continues to be this project's binding constraint**
(§6). The machine was never idle: load never fell below ~7 and stood at **42**
at close, with two unrelated `python3` jobs at 450–490% CPU. D1 discarded a
self-A/B of byte-identical binaries that reported **+62.6%**; D3 declined to
rest on wall clocks at all and reports in-process attributions and paired A/Bs;
the review fixer had edits silently reverted on disk under it by a concurrent
writer; the root filesystem hit 100% once. This continues M12b.md §7's list.

## 2. D1 — the buffer, and the four directions that were not there

*Detail: `docs/status/M12d-D1.md`. §2 buys the noise floor, §3.1 is the change,
§4 refutes the merge, §6 is the netted A/B.*

**The premise held, which makes D1 the only item here that got to do what it
was asked.** M12b §10 item 3 handed over `shading/patch.rs` as the largest
unattributed engine cost in the corpus, and the shape of the question as a
subdivision depth: 28 368 `fill_path` calls for 16 patches.

**One change carried it.** `Points::boundary_path` allocated and returned a
fresh `BezPath` per cell; it becomes `write_boundary_path(&mut BezPath)` into
one buffer that lives for the whole patch. Netted patch figure
**−22.7% / −25.2% / −18.5%** on `shading_axial_radial`, `shading_tcpdf_030` and
`shading_tensor`; engine half −13.7% / −16.0% / −16.5% forward and
+16.3% / +18.3% / +21.9% with the two binaries' order swapped, which is what
separates a real effect from a load drift. RASTER — the control column, which
cannot move on an engine-only change — stays inside the ±2% floor in both
directions on all four documents.

**The item's more durable output is what it closed.** The brief asked for
*fewer, larger* device calls, which is M12 §3.9's stated condition for
re-opening SIMD. A run-length census over the subdivider's own fill order
answers it: on `shading_tcpdf_030`'s dominant shading **every one of 9 909
cells has a colour no adjacent cell shares**; the best case anywhere is 460 of
3 456 cells in runs of exactly two, a 6.7% ceiling on one document and zero on
the other two. And that is the algorithm, not the corpus — the recursion splits
*precisely when* corner colours differ, so two adjacent surviving leaves
necessarily differ. **Making these spans long is a different rasterizer, not an
optimization of this one.** Three more directions measured to zero, two of them
structurally: no cell in the corpus lies outside the buffer it draws into
(0 of 34 272, 0 of 3 456, 0 of 7 181), and the compiler had already seen through
both the `Option` scaffolding and the duplicated point walk.

Conformance was checked **two ways** and the second is the stronger: the third
commit landed while two sibling agents had work in flight elsewhere in
`pdfrum-render`, so a shared-scoreboard run would no longer have been a
statement about this file. Two `pdfrum-tool` binaries differing only in
`shading/patch.rs`, run with `--png --md5` over the bench corpus plus every
Coons and tensor document in the oracle checkout, **agree on all 202 page
hashes**.

`pattern.rs` remains exonerated and was not revisited. What is left in the file
is arithmetic — `survey` and four `split_cubic`s across 53 348 nodes — plus one
per-cell allocation D1 could not reach, `ExactDevice::fill_path`'s
`t * path.clone()`, which belongs to `pdfrum-raster-exact`.

## 3. D2 — an item that was two-thirds already done, and said so

*Detail: `docs/status/M12d-D2.md`. §0 is the withdrawal, §1 the re-derivation,
§2 the work that was genuinely owed.*

**The brief's first instruction was the thing that turned out to be wrong.**
M12b §10 item 8 and PLAN.md's D2 both said the evidence "comes from a branch
that was reverted, so it should be re-derived before being built on", and named
`scratchpad/croscore-fix/` as the patch to re-apply. The patch is real. The
revert is not. `ba8662f` (the heuristic, both branches, gated on
`is_actual_font_loaded`) and `10905fe` (the `CroscoreDb` decorator) landed on
`main` at 23:31 on 2026-08-29 and are ancestors of HEAD. What made them read as
absent from the register alone: `conformance/scoreboard.json` is stamped 19:37
the same day — **earlier than the commits it already contains** — so its
numbers looked stale when they were current. **Withdrawn in place in `d81ac68`,
in M12b.md §10 item 8 and PLAN.md's D2, per the register's rule that a refuted
claim is corrected beside itself rather than edited away.**

The brief demanded re-derivation before building, and D2 did it anyway, which
is what turned a wasted instruction into a useful one. The heuristic's
mechanism, its five gate conditions and both its branches check out line for
line against `cpdf_font.cpp`. Three details of the fixture did not:

- Arimo's `A` advance is **666** per 1000 em, not 667; the factor is
  506/666 = 0.758630, which rounds to the brief's 0.759. The claim is right and
  its intermediate was one unit off.
- The oracle's chosen face is **Arimo Bold Italic**, not the plain Arimo the
  brief and M12b §10 both name — established by rendering against each of the
  thirty faces in `test_fonts` one directory at a time and matching hashes.
  Our ladder lands on the same face, which is the croscore fix's whole purpose.
- The text is set at 20 pt, not 40; the brief's pixel figures are at the
  suite's 2× device scale.

**The one owed step was the serif bit, and it is paid.** Our
`SystemFontDb::describe` derived style bits from PANOSE — a port of
`CFX_Face::GetFontStyle`, which is XFA-only and whose sole caller is in
`xfa/`. `pdfium_test --font-dir` goes through `CFX_FolderFontInfo::ReportFace`
instead, which sets three bits from two strings and nothing else. Enumerated
both ways over the hermetic directory, **eleven faces carried a serif bit the
oracle gives to none of them** and three carried a fixed-pitch bit the same
way, because the Croscore serif families are spelled `Tinos` and `Gelasio` and
neither contains the word "Serif". The row that settles it independently of
the port question is `Gelasio Bold`, whose PANOSE disagrees with its own
siblings' — a rule that splits one family three ways is scoring on something
other than the family, and serif is the 16-point term in
`FaceInfo::similarity_score`.

**The same probe found a second divergence the register does not mention.**
`ReportFace` builds a face name from name IDs 1 and 2; `fontdb`'s
`FaceInfo::families` prefers ID 16, the typographic family, where a face
declares one. `NotoSansCJKjp-Regular.otf` declares both and they disagree —
`"Noto Sans CJK JP Regular"` against `"Noto Sans CJK JP"` — and the name is
read by four scoring paths including a length bonus that eight characters
decides on its own. `describe` now reads name ID 1 directly.

**Both fixes move 0 of 1675 files, measured per file on `status` and
`tierB.ssim`.** That is the predicted result and it is reported as a result
rather than as a disappointment: M12b §10 item 8 said the divergence "did not
bite this corpus but it is real in `FaceInfo::similarity_score`", and that
re-derives exactly true. **They are paid on the oracle comparison and neither
should ever be credited with a scoreboard number.** Five tests carry the
behaviour instead, one of which asserts all thirty faces of the hermetic
directory rather than only the eleven that were wrong — so it reads as a
comparison against the oracle's enumeration rather than as a list of past
failures.

D2 leaves one latent divergence deliberately unchased and says so:
`describe`'s remaining `OS/2` code-page reads were not audited against
`ReportFace`'s. They looked equivalent in passing, and "looked equivalent in
passing" is recorded as what it is rather than as a clean bill.

## 4. D3 — the premise was wrong about what the document is, and that is what found the cost

*Detail: `docs/status/M12d-D3.md`. §1 corrects the premise, §1.1 is the
attribution, §3 the A/B, §4 refutes the ordering hypothesis.*

**The inherited digest was wrong in three of its four parts, and the item's
result is downstream of correcting it.** `image_en_fqa` was described — in
PLAN.md D3, in M12b-P1.md §9.1 and in M12b §10 item 5 — as "552 small images,
every one a 1-bit mask minified 8.33× in both axes". Instrumented at the
dispatch in `render_image`: **301** draws, not 552 (the 552 is the file's image
*XObject* count, not its draw count); each is a **2×2 RGB image carrying a
non-co-registered `/SMask`**, not a 1-bit stencil; and the 8.33× minification
is real but belongs to the **mask**, which runs 48×85 to 1339×86 samples.

That single correction explains both readings that had made the document look
mysterious. `to_pixmap` totals 0.1 ms because the thing it converts is a 2×2
base — so **M12b-P1.md §9.1's reading was right and the conclusion it drew was
wrong**: the cost was per-pixel throughout, on a buffer `to_pixmap` never sees.
And `Phase::Image` reads zero calls against 905 device draws because
`render_masked_image` sits outside the phase wrapper **and** outside M12 §9.1's
render cache — which is why **73.5% of this render had always fallen into the
profile's unattributed interpretation bucket.** Withdrawn in place in
M12b-P1.md §9.1 and M12 §10.3.

Three costs, all the mask's, two of them in the walk and the larger one in the
build:

| what | measured |
|---|---|
| `separate_mask` copied a coverage plane `ImageMask::Alpha` already holds | **32.0% → 0.008 ms**, now a `Cow::Borrowed` with the walk kept for the two cases that need it |
| the reduction box-filtered four identical copies of one channel | **41.7% → 25.4%**, now `reduce_gray_to` with the expansion moved after the reduction |
| `load_mask_image` ran the float round trip **P1 §7 deleted from `to_pixmap` and could not reach**, because it lives in `pdfrum-page` | the larger half, and it is in the *build* |

**Cold 679.7 → 158.7 ms, −76.7%**; page-graph build −83.7%; warm walk −40.4%;
the two figures' sample sets do not overlap on either half. Conformance
byte-identical after every one of the four commits, and every change is proved
identical **by exhaustive test rather than by measurement**.

**Caching was measured, not assumed, and it is not the lever.** Hashing every
mask's samples over one render finds **276 distinct masks in 301 draws** — a
perfect cache saves 8%. That is M12 §10.3's residue in its exact form: the work
had to get *cheaper*, it could not be *skipped*.

**On `image_bug_718762` the brief's own hypothesis is refuted.** D3 was asked
whether the remaining CMYK-table work is spent on pixels `prescale` then
discards, "in which case the fix is ordering, not arithmetic". Enumerating the
tap tables on the real file: the draw is 5000×5000 reduced to 64×64, the
reduction is an area average, and the filter reads **5000 of 5000 source
columns** and every source row. No pixel `to_pixmap` converts is one `prescale`
discards; there is nothing to reorder. The other half of the question —
averaging before converting — was already settled against by P1 §8.3 on oracle
evidence, and that verdict stands. What *was* reachable is the row walk's own
index, in front of the four-dimensional table: `to_pixmap` **409.9 → 367.3 ms,
−10.4%**, the two sets not overlapping, measured by clocking `to_pixmap` itself
rather than the render around it. That is ~42 ms of the ~403 ms P1 left, and
D3 says plainly which half it is: **the remaining ~360 ms is the `scale_denom`
decode and it is upstream's.**

### 4.1 What D3 concluded about the +2 to +6.5% on four other image files

The class table (D3 §3.1) shows four files moving the wrong way —
`image_bug_583804` +2.1%, `image_jbig2_1478366` +2.2%, `image_jbig2_880920`
+2.8%, `image_bug_898443` +6.5%. **D3's conclusion is that this is the noise
floor, not cost moved from `image_en_fqa` onto its neighbours, and it rests the
conclusion on a mechanism rather than on a spread.** `image_bug_898443` — the
largest of the four — is Flate RGB with no soft mask and no CMYK: **not one of
the four commits can reach a line it executes.** A change that cannot touch a
file cannot slow it down, which is M12b §7's case 1 recognized in advance
instead of published. The two JBIG2 files and `image_bug_583804` are the same
shape. That is why the two figures D3 actually rests on — the build/warm split
and the `to_pixmap` clocking — are both measured *inside a process*, and why
the wall-clock class table is reported beside them rather than instead of them.

## 5. The cross-vendor review — new this milestone, and worth repeating

*Both reports verbatim in `docs/reviews/`, each under a header naming the
commit that answered each finding and what was corrected in the report rather
than in the code.*

Two reviews were commissioned from Grok (xAI) against the two bodies of work
this project had least outside scrutiny of: **M12c's GPU backend** and **M12b
P3's walk-performance commits**. Both returned **MERGEABLE-WITH-NITS**, both
were acted on in full, and **nothing was declined**. The reviews are committed
next to the code they judge (`31db224`) rather than left in a scratchpad,
because a review nobody can check the next change against is not a review.

### 5.1 The GPU review found three real should-fixes

None of the three is a performance question and all three are the kind a
same-vendor reviewer of one's own milestone is least likely to raise.

1. **A device leaked before the check that would have refused it.**
   `request_adapter` opened a `Device` and `Queue` and *then* asked
   `try_real_gpu` whether the adapter was real hardware. On any machine whose
   only adapter is llvmpipe — an ordinary CI container, which
   `force_fallback_adapter: false` does not exclude, because llvmpipe
   enumerates as a plain Vulkan adapter — every one of the fourteen GPU tests
   leaked a device on its way to *skipping*. The report and the refusal now
   both happen on the adapter, before `request_device`, and what remains is
   bounded to one device per process behind a `OnceLock` instead of one per
   call.
2. **`Error::TargetTooLarge` was documented and never constructed.** The branch
   that should have built it returned `Pixmap::new(w, h)` instead — for a
   request between the adapter's `max_texture_dimension_2d` and the trait's
   65535 that is **up to seventeen gibibytes of zeros allocated on the way to
   reporting failure**. `check_target` and `try_new_target` now construct the
   variant; the infallible `new_target` clamps, which makes the huge-blank
   branch unreachable by construction.
3. **A lost device panicked.** wgpu's default uncaptured-error handler is
   `panic!`, and a driver reset arrives through it rather than as a `Result` —
   so a display driver restarting would have aborted a host application through
   a PDF library, against STYLE §3. `new` now installs a handler recording the
   first fault into an `Arc<Mutex<_>>` shared with the backend, surfaced by
   `try_finish`, `try_snapshot` and `device_fault`, and checked after the round
   trip as well as before it.

All three in `7af8b95` — one commit, because all three land in `lib.rs` and a
path-scoped commit cannot split a file. The three nits landed separately:
`5ebd4f9` (a readback loop busy-spinning on `PollType::Poll`, now parked in the
driver with a bounded `Wait`), `5dc92f6` (`check-no-wgpu.sh` walking only
`crates/*/`, so `conformance/` and `benches/` — the two likeliest places for
the accident it exists to catch — went unasked; coverage 18 crates → **21**,
all clean, verified again in §7), and `b9a03b3` (no GPU test for a nested mask
or a masked layer popped with no draws, both of which M12c §4.2 reasons about
and never rendered).

**One correction to the report rather than a decline.** Finding 2 offers a
"tiny blank" as the alternative fix. `RasterBackend` has no failure channel and
three CPU backends share it, so the trait method must still return a `Pixmap`
its caller can compose with; the fix is a fallible check *beside* an
infallible clamp, not a blank.

### 5.2 The walk review's NaN finding was real and ran the opposite direction from its reasoning

This is the more instructive of the two, and it is the reason a review is worth
measuring rather than merely applying.

The report reasons that `f64::min`/`max` drop NaN, so a mixed NaN/finite path's
**outer** box goes finite and could **cull** a path the exact test keeps —
conditional, in its own words, on "if kurbo's extrema box stays NaN". **It does
not.** kurbo's extrema solve drops a NaN exactly as the fold does, so a NaN
*control* point leaves both boxes finite and both answers equal, and the
predicted failure cannot occur.

The hole is real and it is the other box and the other kind of point. A NaN on
a drawn **endpoint** lands in the **inner** box unfolded, `outside` compares
with `>` and `<`, every comparison against a NaN is false — so
`!outside(inner, cull)` answered **keep** for every clip on the page while
`path_bbox` answered cull. **A missed cull rather than a false one, so nothing
was ever drawn incorrectly**, which is the difference between a review finding
and a bug report. The fixer measured the disagreement over a sliding clip
before fixing it, rather than reasoning about it a second time — and the fix
the report prescribes (`None` on any non-finite coordinate) closes it either
way, which is why it landed as prescribed.

**Re-measured for this document**, by reconstructing the pre-`8bb811c` bracket
against the committed test's own grid — six shapes, two matrices, 5 122
clip positions each: **5 056 disagreements, every one of them on the path
carrying a bare NaN endpoint**, and **zero** on the NaN *control* point the
report predicted would fail. The NaN-*midpoint* path is zero too, which is the
sharpest form of the correction: once a finite point follows the NaN, kurbo's
segment box folds it away exactly as `min`/`max` do, and the two agree again.
So the report's mechanism is not merely the wrong direction — it is the one
input shape that never disagreed. (A figure of "1 395 disagreements" circulated
in this milestone's handoff; **it is recorded nowhere in the repository and this
reconstruction does not reproduce it.** 5 056 is what the committed test's grid
yields; the count depends entirely on the grid chosen, and the load-bearing
facts are the direction and the shape, not the total.)

The guard is written on finiteness
rather than on which box a point reaches, because the symmetry that makes
control points harmless is a property of today's kurbo and not a promise.

`8bb811c` carries both nit 1 and nit 3, because the test nit 3 asks for is the
test that proves nit 1's fix; without the guard that test's own loop fails
before reaching the assertions that name the mechanism. `71b6e1f` fixed nit 2,
a `Phase::PathXform` doc still claiming two `BezPath` builds per fill after
`d51ef60` fused them — small, and exactly the kind of stale comment that
misdirects the next person reading the numbers that phase produces.
Conformance byte-identical over all 1675 files: **no corpus document carries a
non-finite path coordinate**, which is what keeps this a review finding.

### 5.3 The runner's failure mode, which is worth recording

Both Grok sessions **errored at turn-end after producing their report**. The
work was done and the delivery channel failed. Recovery was to have the runner
write the report **to a file** instead of returning it as a message, which
worked on both. Anyone commissioning a third review should ask for a file
first rather than discovering this twice more.

## 6. Measurement integrity, continued from M12b §7

M12b.md §7 recorded four near-false findings and the mechanism of each, on the
argument that a future reader who sees a surprising number on this machine
needs to recognize the shape. **The machine did not improve and the list
continues.** Load average never fell below ~7 for the whole milestone and stood
at **42** at close; two unrelated `python3` jobs held 450–490% CPU throughout,
one of them for more than fourteen hours. None is an agent.

**5. A self-A/B of byte-identical binaries reporting +62.6%** (D1 §2). D1's
first protocol was seven rounds of twenty iterations, the statistic P3 used.
Its self-A/B — one binary copied under two names — put `shading_axial_radial`'s
engine half at 9.388 ms against 15.269 ms. A load spike landed inside the
second variant's seven rounds and **a minimum over seven samples has no defence
against one bad window when the window is the whole run.** Everything measured
at that protocol was discarded, including the item's first real A/B. At twenty
rounds of fifteen, under a load average of **55** — worse than the first run's
— the same self-A/B gives +1.8% and −0.3%. *Mechanism: too few samples against
a machine with spikes.* **The floor was bought before any result was read**, and
buying it is what let D1 report three wins and four zeros rather than seven
numbers.

**6. Two candidate wins refused on the control column** (D1 §3.2, §5.3). D1's
`Option`-scaffolding change read −4.2% on one document — and **−2.2% in RASTER
on the same run**, on a change that cannot touch the rasterizer. The fused
point walk read +1.2% in one run and −3.2% in the other, the sign following the
*second variant measured* rather than the code, while the load drifted from 114
down to 27 across the window. *Mechanism: a paired A/B that alternates is
protected against a load spike but **not** against a monotone load drift — the
variant sampled later gets the quieter machine on every round.* **Both changes
were kept and neither claim was made.** The generalization is D1's and it is
the useful one: **RASTER is a control column on any engine-only change; it must
stay flat, and when it does not, the engine figure beside it is carrying the
same drift.**

**7. Edits silently reverted on disk by a concurrent writer.** During the
review-fix pass, changes written to files in `crates/pdfrum-render/` were found
absent from disk afterwards, with no error from any tool. Another agent was
writing in the same tree. They were re-applied and verified by reading back.
*Mechanism: a shared working tree with more than one writer.* **This is not a
measurement error and it is more dangerous than one**, because nothing reports
it: a test suite run after a silent revert passes on the old code and says so
honestly. D1 hit the same hazard from the other side and worked around it by
gating its third commit on a two-binary hash comparison rather than on the
shared scoreboard (§2).

**8. The root filesystem hit 100%.** Cleared by D2 (~3 GB, build artefacts). It
stands at 93% as this is written, with 131 GB free. Worth a glance before a
long bench run, because a full disk during one produces failures that look like
the code's.

**The lesson M12b drew still holds and is now better supported: on this
machine, an A/B's consistency is not evidence — only its mechanism is.** Every
number this milestone rests on is either an in-process attribution, a paired
A/B run in both orders, or a byte-identity check. Three of the milestone's
candidate wins were refused by a control column or a self-A/B, and none of them
reached a status document as a result.

## 7. M1 and M2 — the two measurement debts, restated as owed

**Neither was paid, and this milestone is the second in a row to hand them
on.** They were gated in PLAN.md on an idle machine rather than on work, and
the machine was never idle: **load never dropped below ~7 during the milestone
and stood at 42 at close**, with two unrelated `python3` jobs at 450–490% CPU,
one running more than fourteen hours. Neither is an agent and neither cleared.

**No item spent them anyway, and that is the discipline being kept rather than
a convenience.** D1 declined *both* `ratchet update` and `ratchet check` —
`check` reads `target/criterion`, which needs a `cargo bench --workspace`
first, and taking that sweep under a load average ranging from 13 to 114 would
have manufactured exactly the reading the debt exists to prevent. D2 claims no
