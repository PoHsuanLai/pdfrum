# M12b P2 — the page-graph walk, profiled; and the `bumpalo` question, settled

**Status: in progress.** Written incrementally, in M12.md's register: hypothesis,
method, A/B, verdict — including the non-results, which here are the point. This
milestone item exists to *close* a dependency question, and a closed question
with a negative answer is the deliverable just as much as an admitted crate is.

## 0. The starting condition, restated so the conclusion can be judged against it

M12 §3.1 measured the engine half of a render at 54–85% and stopped there,
deliberately. Its instrumentation splits a render at the **engine/rasterizer
seam** — every `RenderDevice` call is clocked, and everything else is "engine" by
subtraction — so "85% is engine" is one number covering three different things:

- **allocation churn** — the `Vec`s, `BezPath`s and `RenderOptions` clones the
  walk builds and drops per object;
- **colour conversion** — `resolve_argb` and the transfer function, per object;
- **interpretation** — the dispatch, the cull test, the clip resolution, the
  glyph placement arithmetic.

DEPS.md gates `bumpalo` on an A/B against a tuned no-dep baseline. An arena only
answers the first of those three. So the profile has to separate them first, and
that is what §2 below does.

## 1. `perf` — asked for, not available; the fallback is the default path

`kernel.perf_event_paranoid` is **4** on this machine. `perf` is installed at
`/usr/bin/perf` and `scripts/profile.sh` detects the sysctl and degrades rather
than failing (its header comment explains both). Privilege escalation is out of
scope for an agent, so:

> **Request to the user (one line, optional):** `sudo sysctl kernel.perf_event_paranoid=1`
>
> With that set, `scripts/profile.sh render benches/corpus/<file>.pdf 50 exact`
> records a real symbol-level profile with a flamegraph, and every number below
> can be cross-checked against a sampler instead of against instrumentation.
> **It was not granted during this session and nothing here depends on it.**

Path (b) — finer-grained instrumentation *inside* the walk — is what M12 §7
specified as the fallback and is what this document uses. It is dep-free, works
under any paranoid setting, needs no `unsafe` (a `GlobalAlloc` shim would need
it, and `unsafe_code = "forbid"` is workspace-wide including `benches/`), and it
stays in the tree afterwards as permanent introspection rather than being deleted
with the measurement.

## 3. The instrument: `walkprofile`, and why it is shaped this way

`crates/pdfrum-render/src/walkprofile.rs`, behind a **default-off** cargo
feature `walk-profile`. With the feature off every entry point is an empty
`#[inline]` function and the module contributes nothing to a build; with it on, a
thread-local `Cell<Profile>` accumulates two kinds of fact:

- **per-phase time** — `clip`, `color`, `glyphs`, `image`, `shading`,
  `path prep`. Each names a span of the walk rather than a function, because
  several are reached from more than one call site.
- **per-site allocation** — count and bytes at each place the walk allocates
  something that dies with the object being drawn. That set is enumerable by
  reading the walk, and it is exactly what an arena could serve.

Two design notes, both load-bearing for reading the numbers.

**A thread-local, not a threaded `&mut`.** STYLE §1 forbids global state and this
is a deliberate, feature-gated exception rather than a quiet one. The alternative
was a `&mut Profile` through the walk's fourteen functions, every one of which
already carries eight or nine arguments and three of which carry a
`#[expect(clippy::too_many_arguments)]` — the instrument would have taken the
shape of the code it measures, which is the one thing an instrument must not do.
It is a `Cell`, not a `RefCell`, because the walk is re-entrant (a form's objects
are walked from inside the enclosing object's phase) and a `RefCell` would panic
on the second borrow.

**Counters, not a sampling allocator.** A `GlobalAlloc` shim answers "how many
bytes went through the allocator" for the whole process in one stroke, and needs
`unsafe impl`. `unsafe_code = "forbid"` is workspace-wide — `benches/` carries it
too — and DEPS.md is closed against a crate that has it. So the sites are counted
where they are, which is less general and, for this question, sufficient.

**The feature is off by default and must stay that way.** Cargo unifies features
across a workspace build, so a default-on forward from `pdfrum-bench` would put
the instrument inside `pdfrum-render`'s own criterion groups and make the
committed numbers measure the timers. `benches/Cargo.toml` says so at the feature.

### The instrument perturbs, and by how much

`vector_paths_1751` measures 4.723 ms/iter with the feature off and 6.271 ms with
it on — **+33%**, all of it the 5010 extra `Instant::now()` pairs the `path prep`
phase adds on a document that is 5010 path objects. Every number in §4 is a
*share*, read from a run with the feature on; every number in §5 onward is an
absolute A/B, read from runs with the feature **off**. Mixing the two would
attribute the instrument's own cost to a change.

### The three-way split, stated plainly

**Colour conversion is not the cost. Anywhere.** It is 7.2% of the engine half on
the one document where it is visible at all and under half a percent on the other
three. That closes one of M12 §7's three candidates outright — no A/B needed,
because there is nothing there to win.

**Allocation churn is concentrated, not diffuse, and it is not where an arena
helps.** Three of the four documents allocate essentially *nothing* per object in
the walk: `vector_paths_1751` makes one allocation per render outside the
zero-area scan, `shading_axial_radial` thirty-three, `text_foxittext` one. The
churn that exists is two specific sites:

- **`text_foxittext`: 2891 glyph outline clones, 3.3 MiB per render** — a whole
  `BezPath` copied out of the glyph cache into every `PlacedGlyph`, on a document
  whose glyphs all take the *bitmap* path, where `PlacedGlyph::outline` is never
  read. This is not arena-shaped. It is a copy that should not happen at all.
- **`vector_paths_1751`: 9866 zero-area vectors** — `zero_area_sub_paths` builds
  a `Vec<SubPath>` and one `Vec<Point>` per sub-path for every fill-only path
  object, scans them, and discards them; on this document the scan finds nothing
  4925 times out of 4925. Arena-shaped in principle, and §6 measures what it is
  actually worth.

**Interpretation is the residue and it is the largest single bucket on three of
the four documents.** On `vector_paths_1751` it is 90% of a 70%-engine render —
and the same document's allocation column is one allocation. That single pairing
is the strongest datum in this document against the arena hypothesis, and §7
records it as such.

One number outside this milestone item's scope, written down as PLAN.md's M12b
asks: `shading_axial_radial`'s ENGINE half is 39% shading evaluation and 61%
residue across only 17 shading draws and 33 clips — the residue is the *pattern*
machinery in `pattern.rs`, not the walk. That is neither P2's territory nor P3's;
it is recorded here so the next milestone has it.

## 5. The no-dep baseline, part one: the zero-area scan's buffers

**Hypothesis.** `zero_area_sub_paths` is 9866 allocations per render of
`vector_paths_1751` — a `Vec<SubPath>` plus one `Vec<Point>` per sub-path, built
on every fill-only path object, scanned, and dropped. It finds nothing 4925 times
out of 4925 on that document. Reusing the memory should be worth something on the
one document where it is measurable and nothing anywhere else.

**Method.** `crates/pdfrum-render/src/zero_area.rs` grows a
[`Scratch`] record — two buffers, a `Vec<Point>` for one sub-path at a time and a
`Vec<ZeroArea>` for the detections — and `scan_into` fills them in place and
returns a borrow. It lives on `RenderCaches` beside the glyph and image caches,
threaded to `draw_path` as one more parameter. Nothing is *remembered* between
paths (the scan's answer depends on the path and caching it would be wrong); what
is reused is the memory. This is exactly the "extend `RenderCaches` buffer reuse
to the walk's per-object temporaries" DEPS.md's ordering requires **before** an
arena may be A/B'd.

The change is pinned by a test that keeps the allocating implementation in the
test body as its reference — not as a wrapper around the new code, which would be
circular — and requires the two to agree on a leading `ClosePath` boundary, two
consecutive `MoveTo`s, a curve (whose flag suppresses two of the three
detectors), a bare `MoveTo`, an empty path, and both `adjust` arms under two
transforms, all against **one** scratch reused across every case.

**A/B**, best of five (nine on the three vector documents), warm, `exact`
backend, `--sample` totals, instrumentation feature **off**:

| Document | before | after | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `vector_paths_1751` | 4.216 ms | **3.806 ms** | **-9.7%** |
| `vector_en_tem` | 6.132 | 6.047 | -1.4% |
| `vector_tcpdf_009` | 15.024 | 15.079 | +0.4% |
| `text_foxittext` | 2.629 | 2.655 | +1.0% |
| `text_tcpdf_055` | 52.587 | 52.400 | -0.4% |
| `text_cjk_page` | 10.421 | 10.583 | +1.6% |
| `mixed_tcpdf_045` | 5.259 | 5.233 | -0.5% |
| `mixed_en_uicase` | 69.174 | 67.999 | -1.7% |
| `shading_axial_radial` | 34.370 | 35.051 | +2.0% |
| `forms_widgets_407` | 0.197 | 0.201 | +2.0% |

**Verdict: kept, at -9.7% on one document and nothing anywhere else** — which is
exactly what the profile predicted, because `vector_paths_1751` is the only
document in the ten whose walk allocated. The other nine are inside the noise
band in both directions and are reported as such rather than averaged into a
geomean that would round the one real number down. The instrument confirms the
mechanism directly: the walk's allocation total on that document goes **9866 → 0**
per render.

**Conformance: byte-identical.** 1675 files, 1624 pass, 51 fail — every entry of
the scoreboard equal to the committed one field for field, with only
`generated_at` differing. Not merely regression-free, which is the weaker rule
the gate enforces. Measured in a clean `git worktree` off this milestone's own
commit with its own target directory, because a second agent is working in the
same checkout on M12b P1 and its uncommitted `pdfrum-page` decode changes would
otherwise have been in the binary under test.

## 6. The no-dep baseline, part two: the glyph outline that was copied and never read

**Hypothesis.** `place_glyphs` builds one `PlacedGlyph` per glyph and each carries
`outline: BezPath`, copied out of the glyph cache. On `text_foxittext` that is
**2891 `BezPath` clones and 3.3 MiB per render** — and every glyph on that page
takes the *bitmap* path, where `PlacedGlyph::outline` is read only on a bitmap
cache **miss**. After the first render of a page the field is dead weight,
allocated and dropped 2891 times.

**Why the copy was there, and what it is not.** It is not carelessness: a
placement record cannot borrow the cache, because placing the *next* glyph needs
the cache mutably again. `cache.path` hands out a `&BezPath` whose lifetime ends
at the next call, so the only way to keep an outline across the loop was to own
one. The fix is to change what the cache stores rather than what the caller does:
`GlyphCache` now holds `Option<Arc<BezPath>>` and grows `shared()` beside `path()`,
so `PlacedGlyph::outline` is an `Arc<BezPath>` and the per-glyph cost is a
refcount bump. `path()` keeps its signature and its callers.

This is the second half of the tuned no-dep baseline DEPS.md requires before an
arena may be A/B'd — and it is worth saying that **an arena would not have fixed
this at all**. An arena makes an allocation cheaper; what was wrong here is that
the allocation is a *copy of data that already exists*, and a bump allocator
copies it just as fast as `malloc` does.

**A/B**, interleaved — the two binaries alternate on each file, best of seven
each, so a load spike lands on both arms rather than on one. That mattered: the
machine carried a load average near 20 from two other agents building in the same
checkout, and a sequential A/B put a +10.4% on a document with no text in it that
re-measured at -2% ten minutes later. The interleaved run is the one reported.

| Document | before | after | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `text_foxittext` | 2.681 ms | **2.519** | **-6.0%** |
| `text_cjk_page` | 10.390 | **9.820** | **-5.5%** |
| `text_bug_1029` | 0.278 | **0.266** | **-4.3%** |
| `mixed_tcpdf_045` | 5.331 | **5.209** | **-2.3%** |
| `text_tcpdf_055` | 51.435 | 51.385 | -0.1% |

**Verdict: kept.** Four documents move by more than the noise band and every one

goes **3955 KiB → 503 KiB** per render. `text_tcpdf_055` is the interesting
non-mover — it is a text document that does *not* improve, because its glyphs are
large enough to take the outline path, where the outline is actually read.

**Conformance: byte-identical** again — all 1675 entries equal to the committed
scoreboard field for field.

## 7. The no-dep baseline, part three: the last per-object allocation

After §5 and §6 the walk's remaining allocation traffic is **one thing**: the
`Vec<PlacedGlyph>` `place_glyphs` returns, one per *text object*. Measured across
all 44 corpus documents, that site is the whole of the top of the table —
`text_tcpdf_055` at 3654 of them per render carrying 23.6 MiB, `text_foxit_products`
1071 carrying 13.6 MiB, `text_tcpdf_063` 725 carrying 3.6 MiB. Every other site is
zero or single digits.

So the same move again: `place_glyphs_into` fills a buffer the caller owns,
`RenderCaches` holds it, and `render_text` lends it out with `std::mem::take` —
`take` rather than a borrow because the loop needs `caches` mutably again for the
bitmap cache and the zero-area scratch, and two mutable borrows of one record do
not coexist. `place_glyphs` survives as this with a fresh `Vec`, which is what
`clip.rs`'s text-clip path still wants.

The instrument needed a correction to report this honestly, and it is worth
recording because it nearly produced a wrong number. Counting *calls* to
`place_glyphs_into` left the allocation column at 3654 after the reuse landed —
the calls still happen, the allocations do not — so the site now counts only a
growth past the capacity the buffer arrived with. On that reading
`text_tcpdf_055` goes **3696 → 42 allocations and 23 650 KiB → 5.9 KiB** per
render, and the 42 that remain are the `RenderOptions`/`RenderCtx` stack moves,
which are not heap traffic at all.

**A/B**, interleaved, best of thirteen, on the four documents where the site was
largest:

| Document | before | after | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `text_foxit_products` | 15.725 ms | 15.119 | -3.9% |
| `vector_font_feature` | 14.857 | 14.281 | -3.9% |
| `text_tcpdf_055` | 51.419 | 51.115 | -0.6% |
| `text_tcpdf_063` | 46.487 | 46.361 | -0.3% |

and on the wider set at best of seven, every document inside the noise band in
both directions (`text_foxittext` -1.1%, `mixed_en_uicase` -0.5%,
`vector_paths_1751` -0.4%, `mixed_tcpdf_045` +0.5%, `shading_axial_radial` +0.4%).

**Verdict: kept, and reported as a non-result, not as a win.** Two documents move
about 4% and two barely move; nothing clears DEPS.md's 10%-on-a-class bar. It is
kept on the same footing M12 §3.7 kept the no-dep `FxHasher` and §9.5 kept the
allocation-free bbox — it is strictly less work, it is no harder to read, and the
scoreboard is byte-identical — **not** because the measurement earned it.

**And this is the measurement the arena question actually turns on.** Removing
3654 allocations and 23 MiB of allocator traffic per render, on the document where
that traffic was largest in the whole corpus, moved the render by 0.6%. Whatever a
bump allocator would do to those same allocations, it cannot do more than delete
them, and deleting them is what was just measured.

## 8. `bumpalo` — the A/B, and the verdict

### 8.1 The dependency itself clears every gate except the one that matters

Checked before measuring anything, because a dep that fails the pure-Rust
guarantee needs no benchmark:

- `bumpalo 3.20.3`, **zero transitive dependencies** — `cargo tree -e normal`
  is two lines. No build script, no `.c`/`.cpp` in the package, nothing for
  `cargo-deny`'s `cc`/`bindgen`/`cmake`/`pkg-config` ban to catch, no `-sys`
  anywhere. `MIT OR Apache-2.0`, on the allowlist. It carries its own audited
  `unsafe`; we would write none, so `forbid(unsafe_code)` stays intact.

So the pure-Rust guarantee is **not** what declines it. The measurement is.

### 8.2 The design constraint, checked first

PLAN.md's P2 says arena lifetimes must never appear in a public type, and that if
they would, the design is disqualified on that alone. They would.

The arena has to live where the walk can reach it across objects, which is
`RenderCaches`. `RenderCaches` is `pub` in `pdfrum-render`, held as
`pub caches: RenderCaches` on the facade's `RenderSession`, which is `pub use`d
from `pdfrum` and is required to be `Send` — `crates/pdfrum/src/lib.rs` asserts
it, and the documented rayon idiom is `map_init(RenderSession::new, ...)`. A
`Bump` on that record makes it `RenderCaches<'a>` and `RenderSession<'a>`, and
the lifetime propagates out through the facade to every caller. (`Bump` itself is
`Send` and `!Sync`, so the `Send` requirement would survive; the lifetime is what
does not.)

That is already disqualifying under the stated rule. It is recorded first and
then set aside, because a design objection is a weaker thing to close a
dependency question with than a number, and the number exists.

### 8.3 The A/B

**What is being compared.** Not "arena versus today's code" — DEPS.md's bar is
against a *tuned no-dep baseline*, and §5–§7 built it. The three arms are the
allocation pattern the walk actually has, at the three shapes the corpus actually
presents (objects x glyphs-per-object taken from the instrument):

- **fresh** — a new `Vec` per object, which is what shipped before §7;
- **reused** — one buffer cleared and refilled, which is what ships now;
- **arena** — a `bumpalo::collections::Vec` per object out of a `Bump`, reset at
  the page boundary rather than per object, because a walk cannot reset per
  object: the arena is borrowed by the vector it just handed out.

Best of fifteen, release build:

| Shape (from the instrument) | fresh | reused | arena | arena vs **reused** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `text_tcpdf_055` — 3654 obj x 7 glyphs | 0.138 ms | **0.040** | 0.144 | **+265%** |
| `text_foxit_products` — 1071 x 30 | 0.093 | **0.049** | 0.109 | **+121%** |
| `text_foxittext` — 59 x 49 | 0.007 | **0.005** | 0.009 | **+97%** |

**The arena is slower than the tuned baseline on every shape, and slower than the
untuned one too.** The mechanism is not subtle and is worth stating because it
generalizes: a bump allocator is fast at *handing out* memory and has nothing to
say about *touching* it. The reuse path writes the same few kilobytes on every
object, and after the first object that memory is in L1. The arena hands each
object a fresh, cold region and cannot reset until the page ends — resetting per
object is what the borrow checker forbids, since the arena is borrowed by the
vector it is filling. So the arena trades a warm buffer for a cold one and pays
for it.

### 8.4 The number that makes the microbenchmark's verdict the real one

The scale above is the whole argument, and it is why §7's in-tree A/B is the
authority rather than this table. **The entire allocation cost of the walk's
worst document, in the worst of the three arms, is 0.14 ms against a 51 ms
render.** §7 measured the real thing directly and agrees: deleting 3654
allocations and 23 MiB of allocator traffic per render — on the document where
that traffic was largest in all 44 — moved the render by **0.6%**.

A bump allocator cannot do more to an allocation than make it free. Making these
free was measured, in the tree, on the real renderer, and it is worth 0.6%. The
bar is 10% on a class or 5% on the geomean.

### 8.5 Verdict: NOT ADMITTED

`bumpalo` is declined. Not because it is impure — it is the cleanest dependency
this project has evaluated, with no transitive dependencies at all — and not
primarily because of the lifetime, though the lifetime alone disqualifies the
design under PLAN.md's rule. It is declined because **allocation is not the
engine half's cost**, and the profile that M12 §7 said was the prerequisite is
what establishes that:

- Colour conversion: **7.2% at most, under 0.5% on three of four documents.**
- Allocation churn: **now 42 allocations per render on the worst document, down
  from 3696, worth 0.6%.**
- Interpretation: **the residue, and the majority of the engine half on three of
  four documents** — 90.3% on `vector_paths_1751`, 68.0% on `text_foxittext`,
  60.8% on `shading_axial_radial`.

M12 §9.5 called its bbox result "the first data point against allocation being
the engine half's cost" and declined to conclude from it. This is the profile it
asked for, and it says the same thing with numbers on every site: the walk's
allocation churn was real, it was removable without a dependency, removing it was
worth a few percent on four documents, and what is left is interpretation.

**The arena row in DEPS.md is closed.** The reopening condition, stated so it is
falsifiable: a workload where the walk's *own* allocation traffic — not the
rasterizer's, not the decoder's — is more than a few percent of a render after
`RenderCaches` reuse. Nothing in the 44-document corpus is such a workload; the
largest is 0.6%.

## 9. What the walk's real cost is, and where it goes next

The profile's third bucket is the one left standing, and P2's remit ends at
naming it: **interpretation is 60–90% of the engine half**, and after §5–§7 it is
essentially all of it. It is not one function. On `vector_paths_1751` the engine
half is 3.98 ms across 5010 objects — **794 ns per object** — spent on the
dispatch `match`, the cull test, `GraphicsState` reads, the `RenderCtx`
threading, and `draw_path`'s five-case decision tree. There is no allocation left
in it and no colour conversion worth the name.

That is a different kind of target from anything M12 or this item attacked. It is
not a loop to vectorize or a buffer to reuse; it is per-object constant overhead,
and the way to move it is to do less per object — which means the cull test, the
`Copy`-ness of the state records the walk reads, and whether `draw_path`'s five
tests can be decided once per *object* rather than once per call. **It is deferred
deliberately rather than attempted badly**: it is a design question about the
walk's shape, and this item's mandate was to find out where the cost is, not to
restructure the engine on the last afternoon of it.
