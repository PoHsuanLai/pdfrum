# M12d D1 — `shading/patch.rs` gets its owner

**Status: complete.** (§8 has the three commits; this header was left at "in
progress" when they landed.)

The Coons (type 6) and tensor (type 7) mesh rasterizer was in no measurement
bucket at all until M12b P3 gave it `Phase::Patches` (`1790568`). This item is
the owner P3 named and M12b §10 item 3 recorded as owed. It is a **pure
performance** item: the conformance scoreboard must come out byte-identical,
per file, not merely free of regressions.

## 0. The measurement conditions, stated before any number

The machine is not idle. `uptime` reports a load average of **13.2 on 32
cores** for the whole of this item, from unrelated jobs. Every number below is
therefore a **paired A/B taken by alternating the two binaries round by round
inside one window**, with the minimum per variant — the statistic M12 §3.9 used
under the same contention, and the only one that gives both variants the same
interference. Absolute milliseconds in this document are not comparable to
M12b's; the *ratios* are what it claims.

`ratchet update` is **not run** here: M12b §10 item 1 owes that debt on an idle
machine and it stays owed. `ratchet check` is expected to pass and does.

All A/Bs use `profile --op render --sample`. The plain loop rebuilds the page
graph per iteration and is mostly `pdfrum-page`; P3 §4 records a +5%
"regression" it produced that survived a bisection and did not exist.

## 1. The baseline, and what the netted figure actually is

`--sample`, `exact` backend, 20-30 iterations, `walk-profile` build for the
phase split:

| Document | patch calls | `fill_path` calls | `fill_path` (RASTER) | `patches` (gross) | **netted** | ENGINE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `shading_axial_radial` | 16 | 28 368 | 23.24 ms | 29.34 ms | **6.10 ms** | 9.89 ms |
| `shading_tcpdf_030` | 3 | 51 854 | 37.88 ms | 49.37 ms | **11.49 ms** | 13.94 ms |
| `shading_tensor` | 13 | 16 389 | 9.52 ms | 12.52 ms | **3.00 ms** | 3.08 ms |
| `shading_coons` (control) | **0** | **0** | 0 | **0** | **0** | 0.09 ms |

Two things to read off this before anything is changed.

**The netted figure is larger on this machine than M12b's 2.5 / 6.1 ms**, and
that is the instrument and the contention, not a change in the code. What
survives from M12b is the shape: netted patch work is **essentially the whole**
of each document's engine half. On `shading_tensor` it is 3.00 of 3.08 ms —
97%. On `shading_axial_radial` it is 6.10 of 9.89 ms, and the residue is the
17 axial/radial shadings the same document also carries (`shading` = 3.63 ms).

**The control is a control because it is empty.** `shading_coons` makes zero
patch calls and has a 0.09 ms engine half. Nothing in this item can move it,
and if anything does, the change is wrong.

**The ratio that names the target**: 28 368 `fill_path` calls for 16 patches is
1773 device calls per patch, and M12 §3.9 already measured what those calls
cost downstream — 342 308 solid spans covering 343 088 pixels, a mean span
length of **1.00**. The rasterizer is being handed thousands of sub-pixel
quadrilaterals.

## 2. The noise floor had to be bought before any A/B could be read

The first paired A/B this item ran used **7 rounds** of 20 iterations, the
statistic P3 used. Its self-A/B — the same binary copied under two names, run
through the same alternation — reported:

| Document | ENGINE, A | ENGINE, A2 | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `shading_axial_radial` | 9.388 ms | 15.269 ms | **+62.6%** |
| `shading_tcpdf_030` | 13.454 ms | 13.700 ms | +1.8% |
| `shading_tensor` | 4.315 ms | 4.444 ms | +3.0% |
| `shading_coons` | 0.176 ms | 0.185 ms | +5.1% |

**+62.6% between two byte-identical binaries.** A load spike landed inside A2's
seven rounds and the minimum never recovered from it, because a minimum over
seven samples has no defence against one bad window when the window is the
whole run. Everything measured at 7 rounds is unreadable, and the first real
A/B taken there is discarded rather than reported.

At **20 rounds** of 15 iterations, alternating, under a load average of **55**
— worse than the first run's — the same self-A/B gives:

| Document | ENGINE, A | ENGINE, A2 | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `shading_axial_radial` | 9.239 ms | 9.409 ms | **+1.8%** |
| `shading_tensor` | 2.764 ms | 2.756 ms | **−0.3%** |
| `shading_coons` | 0.082 ms | 0.083 ms | +1.2% (RASTER +4.1%) |

**So the protocol for this item is 20 rounds, and the noise floor is ±2% on
ENGINE for the two documents that carry patch work.** The control's absolute is
0.08 ms and its floor is ±4%; nothing under 5% on `shading_coons` is a
statement about anything.

This is not a detail. The instruction to measure before believing either
direction cuts both ways: an unbought noise floor would have let this item
publish a −20% that was a scheduling accident.

## 3. The first hypothesis, and the one that carried the item

**Hypothesis.** The netted patch cost is not arithmetic. The census below says
a mesh reaches `fill_path` tens of thousands of times, and the engine side of
each of those calls builds a `BezPath` — six elements, a heap allocation and a
free — and then throws it away. If that is where the time is, the fix is a
buffer, not a formula.

**The census that ranked it.** Counters compiled into `subdivide`, dumped and
reset per shading (so these are per-*shading*, not per-render), on the three
documents that have patches:

| Document | shading | cells filled | ended `small` | ended flat | mean cell bbox |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `shading_tcpdf_030` | the big one | 34 272 | **0** | 34 272 | 3.64 x 3.88 |
| `shading_axial_radial` | each of two | ~3 400 | ~1 200 | ~2 200 | 2.4 x 2.0 |
| `shading_axial_radial` | each of two | 127 | 0 | 127 | 17.3 x 77.1 |
| `shading_tensor` | the big one | 7 181 | 6 088 | 1 093 | 1.4 x 1.3 |

Two facts fall out that are worth more than the hypothesis they were gathered
for.

**The cells are not sub-pixel.** Only 18 of `shading_axial_radial`'s 28 368
cells and **none** of `shading_tcpdf_030`'s are under one device unit in both
axes; the mean cell on `tcpdf_030` is nearly 4x4. The picture of this
rasterizer as one that grinds a patch into dust is wrong — it is one that
issues thousands of *ordinary small fills*.

**And on the largest of them, subdivision stops on colour, not on size.**
`tcpdf_030`'s dominant shading terminates **every one** of its 34 272 cells on
the colour threshold with `small` never once true. `shading_tensor` is the
opposite — 85% end on size. The two documents are different regimes and the
same change has to serve both.

### 3.1 Landed: one path buffer for the whole patch, not one per cell

`Points::boundary_path` allocated and returned a fresh `BezPath` per cell. It
becomes `write_boundary_path(&mut BezPath)`, writing into a buffer that lives
in a new `Cells` record alongside the destination device, the corner colours
and the ramp — the four things constant down the whole recursion. `truncate(0)`
resets it and keeps the capacity, so after the first cell the path is six
pushes into warm memory.

Carrying a buffer meant the recursion's nine parameters had to be regrouped,
and the regrouping is the part worth reading: what was constant became `Cells`,
and the four lattice values that each split rewrites became a `Lattice` record
with the two halvings as its own methods. That deleted the
`clippy::too_many_arguments` allowance the old signature carried and, once the
four-way split reads as two halvings, the `too_many_lines` one as well. The
lattice arithmetic is unchanged — `halve_vertically` doubles `y_scale` and
`bottom` exactly where the old code wrote `y_scale * 2` and `bottom * 2`.

**The paired A/B**, 20 rounds of 15 iterations, alternating, load average 37:

| Document | ENGINE before | ENGINE after | delta | RASTER delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `shading_axial_radial` | 9.487 ms | 8.692 ms | **−8.4%** | −1.2% |
| `shading_tcpdf_030` | 13.107 ms | 11.361 ms | **−13.3%** | −0.9% |
| `shading_tensor` | 4.153 ms | 3.409 ms | **−17.9%** | +2.7% |
| `shading_coons` (control) | 0.099 ms | 0.099 ms | **±0.0%** | −0.5% |

Against a ±2% floor, all three are real. **RASTER is inside the floor on all
four**, which is the check that says the change is what it claims to be: it
removes engine-side allocation and hands the rasterizer the identical geometry,
so the raster half must not move, and it does not. **The control is unmoved to
three decimal places.**

**Conformance: byte-identical.** 1675 files, 1624 pass, 51 fail, and the
scoreboard diff against the committed one is **one line, the timestamp** — the
same numbers, file for file, not merely regression-free.

### 3.2 Landed on its own merits, measured at nothing: the `Option` scaffolding

`bilinear`, `distance` and the boundary walk all reached fixed-size arrays
through `.get(i)`/`.first()` chains, so every one of those subscripts was
statically in bounds and every `Option` statically `Some` — but the `None` arms
were still in the function, on a path that has none, four times per recursion
node. Destructuring by pattern removes them. `Points::bbox` likewise folded
sixteen degenerate `Rect`s through `Rect::union` with an `Option` branch per
point; `extent` is the same four `min`/`max`es as a plain fold, and `is_small`
now takes the extent directly instead of a `Rect` it immediately measures.

**It measures at nothing**, and this is a non-result reported as one:

| Document | ENGINE, §3.1 | ENGINE, plus this | delta | RASTER delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `shading_axial_radial` | 8.665 ms | 8.305 ms | −4.2% | **−2.2%** |
| `shading_tcpdf_030` | 11.917 ms | 11.639 ms | −2.3% | −0.0% |
| `shading_tensor` | 2.284 ms | 2.278 ms | −0.3% | +1.9% |

Two of the three are inside the ±2% floor. The third, `shading_axial_radial`'s
−4.2%, comes with a **−2.2% in RASTER on the same run**, and nothing in this
change touches the rasterizer — so the document's engine figure carries the
same drift its raster figure does, and what is left of the −4.2% is not
separable from the floor. The load average during this pair was **114**.

**The claim is therefore not made.** The change is kept because it is the
better code — it deletes dead `None` arms and a lint allowance rather than
adding any — and because it costs nothing, not because it bought anything. The
optimizer had already seen through the scaffolding.

## 4. "Fewer, larger device calls" — the direction the brief named, refuted by counting

The brief asked for *fewer, larger* device calls per patch, and M12 §3.9's
reopening condition is span length: if the shading rasterizer stopped
decomposing into thousands of small fills, the compositor would become a
long-span walk and the SIMD question could be re-asked. It is the largest idea
available here and it deserved a measurement rather than an argument.

The measurement is a run-length census over the depth-first order the
subdivider fills in: how many *consecutive* cells carry the same final `Argb`,
which is the only shape a merge could take without reordering the paint.

| Document | shading | cells | runs | longest run | cells in runs >= 2 | distinct colours |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `shading_tcpdf_030` | the big one | 9 909 | 9 909 | **1** | **0** | 9 909 |
| `shading_tcpdf_030` | another | 8 791 | 8 791 | **1** | **0** | 8 791 |
| `shading_axial_radial` | typical | 3 456 | 3 226 | **2** | 460 | 251 |
| `shading_tensor` | the big one | 7 181 | 7 181 | **1** | **0** | 7 181 |

**On `shading_tcpdf_030`'s dominant shading every one of 9 909 cells has a
colour no adjacent cell shares.** The best case anywhere in the corpus is
`shading_axial_radial`, where 460 of 3 456 cells sit in a run of exactly two —
so the ceiling on merging is turning 3 456 fills into 3 226, a **6.7%**
reduction in call count, on the one document where the idea works at all, and
zero on the other two.

**And this is not an accident of these files; it is the algorithm.** The
recursion subdivides *precisely when* the corner colours differ by four counts
or more, and stops when they do not. A node that split did so because its
children's colours are distinguishable, so two adjacent surviving leaves
necessarily carry different colours — the merge condition and the split
condition are the same test with opposite signs. The 251 distinct colours on
`shading_axial_radial` against 3 456 cells look like an opportunity until you
notice they are spread across the patch: the same colour recurs far apart in
the sweep, never next to itself.

**So the direction is refused, and the reason is upstream's own decomposition,
which the brief warned might not permit it.** A merge that reordered the paint
to gather like colours would also change what `full_cover` overpaints, which is
the one thing this file is not allowed to move. M12 §3.9's reopening condition
stands unmet and stands for a reason: making these spans long is not an
optimization of this rasterizer, it is a different rasterizer.

**A second thing the same census settles.** The cells are *not* sub-pixel dust:
18 of `shading_axial_radial`'s 28 368 and none of `shading_tcpdf_030`'s are
under one device unit in both axes, and `tcpdf_030`'s mean cell is 3.6 x 3.9.
M12 §3.9 measured a mean *span* length of 1.00 on this document, and it is
worth saying why both are true: a 4x4 quadrilateral rasterized under
`full_cover` is nearly all boundary, so it produces boundary spans of one pixel
each. The spans are short because the cells are small **relative to their
perimeter**, not because the cells are sub-pixel.

## 5. What measured to zero

Two directions were instrumented and produced a flat zero. Both are recorded
because the counting is the result, and neither should be re-derived.

### 5.1 Culling cells outside the scratch — nothing to cull

`subdivide` has no offscreen reject of its own, and `draw_patch`'s
whole-patch one is half a test: it checks `bbox.x1 <= 0.0 || bbox.y1 <= 0.0`
and never the right or bottom edge. A cell wholly outside the scratch still
walks the recursion and still issues a `fill_path` that writes nothing, and the
`exact` backend pays `reset` + `add_path` + a sweep for it. Completing the
reject looked like free money.

Counting the cells that would be culled, against the scratch's own width and
height:

| Document | recursion nodes | nodes wholly outside | cells filled | cells wholly outside | cells partly outside |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `shading_tcpdf_030` | 53 348 | **0** | 34 272 | **0** | **0** |
| `shading_axial_radial` | 5 405 | **0** | 3 456 | **0** | **0** |
| `shading_tensor` | 10 341 | **0** | 7 181 | **0** | **0** |

**Not one cell in the corpus is outside the buffer it draws into, and not one
even crosses its edge.** The reason is upstream in `walk.rs`: the scratch is
allocated at exactly `outer_rect(bbox ∩ device_box)` of the shading being
drawn, so the buffer is fitted to the mesh rather than to the page. The
incomplete reject in `draw_patch` is therefore dead code that happens to be
correct, and completing it would add a test per cell to cull nothing.

### 5.2 The `fill_path` seam's own transform — not ours to take

`ExactDevice::fill_path` opens with `let device_path = t * path.clone()`, and
every call from this file passes `Affine::IDENTITY`. That clone is a second
allocation per cell, on top of the one §3.1 removed. It is also in
`crates/pdfrum-raster-exact/`, which this item does not own, and it is a change
to a device trait's contract rather than to a shading — so it is named here for
whoever does own it, and not attempted.

### 5.3 Fusing the two sixteen-point walks — zero, and a bias worth naming

Every recursion node walked its sixteen control points **twice**: once in
`all_finite` and once in `extent`. On `shading_tcpdf_030` that is 53 348 nodes,
so 1.7 million point visits where 853 000 would do. The two fuse cleanly — a
NaN leaves no trace in a min/max fold (`f64::min` returns its non-NaN operand),
so finiteness has to be tested as the points go past anyway — and the fused
walk returns a `Survey` record carrying both answers.

**It measures at zero, and the second run measured it at zero with the opposite
sign**, which is the part worth keeping:

| | run 1 (HEAD first) | run 2 (fused first) |
|---|---:|---:|
| `shading_tcpdf_030` ENGINE | **+1.2%** | **−3.2%** |
| `shading_tcpdf_030` RASTER | −0.6% | −3.8% |
| `shading_tensor` ENGINE | −0.8% | −0.8% |
| `shading_tensor` RASTER | −2.7% | −1.6% |

`shading_tcpdf_030` flips sign between the two runs, and in **both** of them the
sign follows the *second* variant measured, not the code. The machine's load
average fell from 114 through 33 to 27 across this window, and a paired A/B
that alternates within a window is protected against a load *spike* but not
against a monotone load *drift*: the variant sampled later gets the quieter
machine on every one of its twenty rounds. The RASTER column is what makes this
readable — it moves by as much as ENGINE, in the same direction, on a change
that cannot touch the rasterizer.

**So the fusion buys nothing measurable.** The compiler was almost certainly
already fusing two adjacent loops over the same fixed-size array, which is the
same lesson §3.2 taught. It is kept for the same reason: one walk with a named
result reads better than two anonymous ones, and it costs nothing.

**The methodological point, for whoever measures on this machine next.** Reading
RASTER beside ENGINE is what caught this. RASTER is a control column on any
engine-only change — it *must* stay flat, and when it does not, the engine
figure beside it is carrying the same drift. Two of this item's three candidate
wins were refused on that test alone.

## 6. The item's result, measured in both directions

The three changes together, A/B'd against the pristine file, **run twice with
the order of the two binaries swapped** — because §5.3 established that a
monotone load drift across the window fakes a result on whichever variant is
sampled second, and swapping the order is what separates the two. A real effect
reverses sign and keeps its magnitude; a drift artefact keeps its sign.

| Document | forward, ENGINE | reversed, ENGINE | forward, RASTER | reversed, RASTER |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `shading_axial_radial` | **−13.7%** | **+16.3%** | −3.8% | +1.3% |
| `shading_tcpdf_030` | **−16.0%** | **+18.3%** | −1.4% | +0.8% |
| `shading_tensor` | **−16.5%** | **+21.9%** | −1.5% | +2.9% |
| `shading_coons` (control) | −1.2% | +6.0% | −0.5% | +4.7% |

**All three reverse.** The magnitudes are 13.7/16.3, 16.0/18.3 and 16.5/21.9 —
the reversed run is uniformly a little larger because the load was falling
through both windows and the second variant benefits either way, which is the
§5.3 bias showing up as an asymmetry of a couple of points rather than as the
whole result. **RASTER stays inside the ±2% floor in both directions on all
four documents**, which is the control column: the geometry handed to the
rasterizer is unchanged, so it must not move, and it does not.

**The control does not move either.** `shading_coons`' −1.2%/+6.0% is the same
number as its own RASTER's −0.5%/+4.7% on a 0.08 ms engine half with **zero
patch calls** — it is the noise floor §2 measured at ±4% on this document, not
a signal. Nothing in this item can reach it and nothing did.

### Conformance

**Byte-identical, checked two ways.**

The first two changes were gated on the shared harness: `conformance run
--check-regressions`, 1675 files, 1624 pass, 51 fail, and the scoreboard diff
against the committed one is **one line, the timestamp** — the same numbers,
file for file, which is stricter than the regression gate.

The third was gated **directly**, because by then two other agents had work in
flight elsewhere in `pdfrum-render` and a shared-scoreboard run would no longer
have been a statement about this file. Two `pdfrum-tool` binaries differing
only in `shading/patch.rs`, run with `--png --md5` over the whole bench corpus
plus every Coons and tensor document in the oracle checkout — including
`2_shading_type_6_00`, which this file's own tests name as the one that catches
a lattice-axis swap, and both `bug_481` patches — agree on **all 202 page
hashes**. That is the M12 §3.9 method, and on this file it is the sharper gate.

### What was not run, and why

**`ratchet update` was not run and the baseline is still owed.** M12b §10 item
1 gates it on an idle machine; the load average during this item ranged from 13
to 114. `ratchet check` passes and is expected to. Publishing a re-baseline
from this window would write today's contention into the tree as though it were
the engine's speed.

`scripts/ci.sh` is **green** on the tree as committed — fmt, clippy across the
workspace with warnings denied, 331 nextest cases, doctests, rustdoc, cargo-deny
and both halves of the pure-Rust / GPU-isolation check.

**`ratchet check` could not be run and that is not the same as its passing.**
It reads `target/criterion`, which requires `cargo bench --workspace` first, and
a criterion sweep taken under a load average that ranged from 13 to 114 across
this item would be exactly the number M12b §10 item 1 says must be gathered on
an idle machine. Running the sweep to satisfy the check would have manufactured
the reading the debt exists to prevent. **The ratchet is left untouched: no
`update`, and no `check` result claimed.**

## 7. What is left in `shading/patch.rs`, for whoever takes it next

The netted figure is a fifth to a quarter smaller and it is still 98% of two of
these documents' engine halves. What remains, ranked, with what this item
learned about each:

1. **The recursion itself.** After the buffer, what is left per node is
   `survey`'s one walk of sixteen points and `split_cubic` four times — 53 348
   nodes on `shading_tcpdf_030`, each doing De Casteljau on four cubics. This
   is the residue and it is arithmetic, not overhead. **Do not attack it with
   SIMD**: M12 §3.9 declined that on span length, and §4 above shows the span
   length cannot change while the decomposition stands.

2. **`ExactDevice::fill_path`'s `t * path.clone()`** (§5.2), which is a second
   allocation per cell and belongs to `pdfrum-raster-exact`. It is the one
   remaining per-cell allocation in the whole path and this item could not
   reach it.

3. **Nothing in the four directions this item closed.** Merging adjacent cells
   (§4, refuted by counting — the longest run of same-coloured neighbours is 2,
   usually 1), culling offscreen cells (§5.1, zero of them exist), the `Option`
   scaffolding (§3.2, zero) and fusing the two point walks (§5.3, zero). Three
   of those four are *structurally* zero rather than incidentally zero, and
   §§4, 5.1 give the arithmetic that makes them so.
