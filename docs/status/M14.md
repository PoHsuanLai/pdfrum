## OQ6 — the caret's blink phase in a golden. **Answered: always drawn.**

Settled before any caret code was written, as the ruling asked, by reading one
golden and one C++ file.

`testing/resources/pixel/form_textfield_focused_ltr_expected.pdf.0.png` is
200×100. Its `.evt` types `abc... def` into the field at (100, 50) and
deliberately never clicks away, so the field is focused when the image is
taken. Scanning the image's dark columns:

- columns 51–99 are the glyph run `abc... def`, all antialiased colour;
- **column 100 is a solid `(16, 16, 16)` bar spanning y 38–51** — fourteen
  rows, four taller than the glyph band (y 40–48) at each end, one pixel wide,
  with column 101 a clean `(238, 238, 238)` and column 102 empty.

That is a caret, drawn, immediately after the final `f`. So a focused field's
`LiveEdit` appearance draws one.

The C++ says why, and says it is not luck. `CPWL_Caret::DrawThisAppearance`
returns early on `!flash_`, and `flash_` is set **`true`** the moment the caret
becomes visible (`cpwl_caret.cpp:97`); the only thing that ever clears it is
`OnTimerFired`, on a 500 ms `CFX_Timer`. `pdfium_test` runs no message loop
that fires it — its `idler()` is empty in a V8-off build — and the determinism
recipe freezes the clock at `--time=1399672130` besides. The caret is therefore
lit on every first render, on every platform, and the phase question the brief
flagged as "the one place the goldens might diverge for a reason we do not
control" does not arise.

**Ruling for the implementation: draw the caret unconditionally whenever a
field is focused with an empty selection.** Geometry, from `cpwl_caret.{h,cpp}`:
a line of width `0.4` units (`cpwl_caret.h:41`) stroked at
`x = rect.left + width * 0.5` from the caret rect's bottom to its top, in
opaque black, clipped to the client rect. And it is *selection*-gated, not
focus-gated alone: `SetCaretInfo` passes `selection.is_empty()` as the
visibility flag and `CPWL_Edit::SetCaret` forces invisible when
`!IsFocused() || IsSelected()` — **a field with a live selection shows no
caret** (brief §1.20.5 rule 7). The two `form_textfield_selected_*` goldens are
the other side of that switch.

## U2 — the hit-test tie-break. **Answered: strict `>` against the half-width.**

The ruling assigned U2 to the `pdfrum-doc` slice, and that slice's agent has
now read it and reported the rule; it is recorded here because
`place_at_point`'s callers in this crate are what an error in it would break.

`CPVT_Section::SearchWordPlaceImpl` is a binary search whose only comparison is

```cpp
if (fx > pWord->fWordX + vt_->GetWordWidth(*pWord) * 0.5f)
```

**Strict `>`, against the word's own x plus half its width.** So a click
exactly on a glyph's midpoint lands *before* that glyph, and the answer is the
place after the last word whose midpoint is strictly left of the click — the
word index staying `-1`, the "before the first word" place, when none
qualifies.

The strictness and the `0.5` both matter: the `InsertTextInPopulatedTextField`
family clicks at x = 134 and expects the caret after exactly four characters,
so an off-by-one here fails four assertions at once. Caret-placement tests in
this crate are written around the midpoint rule rather than around a
nearest-edge one.

## Two tab-order seed rules, verified against a compiled simulation

Both are upstream quirks that change the resulting order, and both were
reproduced only after checking a transcription of the C++ against this
crate's output, because reading either one from the source alone invites the
wrong answer.

1. **A row band's seed is the *rightmost* of the annotations tied for
   topmost.** The scan runs from the highest index down with a strict `>`, so
   a tied annotation never displaces the running best, and after the
   left-ascending sort the first one visited is the rightmost. Two rows of two
   therefore traverse `3, 1, 0, 2`, not the `1, 3, 2, 0` reading order
   suggests. Confirmed by compiling the C++ loop verbatim against the same
   fixture.
2. **The column pass's "nothing chosen yet" guard is `left < 0`, and when it
   fires it seeds index zero** rather than the index being examined. On a page
   with negative x coordinates the guard re-fires on later iterations. Both
   halves are reproduced; this is why the row and column passes are not one
   function with a flipped axis.

## The facade, and what a bridge can rely on

`pdfrum::FormSession` is in, with the documented example the exit criterion
asks for, and it is **stable enough to bridge onto**. Three shapes are settled
so the `--send-events` wiring does not have to move later:

- coordinates are **page space**, y-up, with no conversion on the way in;
- there is **no key-up method** — the oracle's is a documented permanent no-op
  returning false, so modelling it would only invite callers to send a dead
  event — and the `.evt` `keycode` verb's down/up pair maps to one `KeyDown`;
- the **right button is reachable** through `on_button` rather than absent,
  because scripts contain right-button lines and the correct behaviour for
  them is to consume nothing.

Mouse and keyboard dispatch currently report every event unhandled, and the
method docs say why rather than leaving a bare stub: routing needs the per-page
annotation geometry and the caret-placement queries. **"Not consumed" is a
promise a bridge can rely on not to change meaning** when routing lands — a
guess would not be.

## Dispatch wiring — `pdfrum-tool --send-events` onto the facade

`--send-events` no longer only parses and counts: `crates/pdfrum-tool/src/dispatch.rs`
bridges `pdfrum_tool::events::Event` onto `pdfrum::FormSession`, and `run.rs`
replays the whole script against every page before that page's image is
written. The result is **inert-correct**: the facade's dispatch reports every
event unhandled by design, so the wiring changes no pixel yet — and that is
the property this slice is scored on.

### The seven bridging facts, and where each one lives

The harness reviewer's list (`docs/reviews/claude-review-evt-harness.md`) is
binding, so each fact is named at the code that implements it rather than only
here.

| # | Fact | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Coordinates are page space, y-up — **no transform** | `dispatch::at`, an `i32`→`f32` widening and nothing else |
| 2 | `keycode` is the down/up pair → **one** `on_key_down`, the up dropped | `dispatch::to_call`'s `KeyCode` arm; `Call` has no `KeyUp` variant to hold one |
| 3 | `CharCode.code` is an `i32` code point, narrowed **fallibly** | `dispatch::to_char`, with the skip announced on stderr by `replay_page` |
| 4 | Modifiers pass through | `dispatch::to_modifiers`, the grammar's three bits onto the semantic ones |
| 5 | Never repair unbalanced mouse events | `to_call` holds no state at all, so there is nothing that *could* synthesize the missing edge |
| 6 | Replay the whole stream once per page, before saving that page's image | `run::walk_pages`, after the page-load guard and ahead of every dump |
| 7 | Goldens are script-keyed already | nothing to do; the harness landed it in `d72e8ac` |

Two decisions inside those worth stating, because a later reader would
otherwise assume the opposite:

- **An invalid code point is skipped, never replaced.** `charcode,55296` is a
  lone surrogate; substituting U+FFFD would type a visible glyph the oracle
  never typed. The skip goes to **stderr**, because stdout is the
  byte-compared stream.
- **`Call` is a named intention, not a direct call.** The facade's dispatch is
  inert, so a test driving a real session cannot tell a correct bridge from one
  that sends nothing. `to_call` is a pure function of the grammar event, which
  is what makes the key-pair collapse, the skipped code point, the untouched
  mouse imbalance and the verb-for-verb ordering all assertable without a
  document. `perform` is a total match over it, so a variant cannot be added
  without a facade call being wired.

### The order reproduced from `pdfium_test`

`PdfProcessor::ProcessPage` (`pdfium_test.cc:1474-1482`) sends events as its
**first** statement, ahead of every dump and every render; a page that would
not load returns before reaching it. `GetPage` (`:871-872`) runs
`FORM_OnAfterLoadPage` then `FORM_DoPageAAction(OPEN)` on first load, and the
render is `FPDF_RenderPageBitmap` with `FPDF_ANNOT` (`:1130`, flags from
`:221`) followed by `PageRenderer::Finish`'s `FPDF_FFLDraw` over the same
bitmap (`:1046`). **`FORM_ForceToKillFocus` is never called** — I grepped the
whole of `testing/pdfium_test/` — which is why the focused fixtures are
expected to render their live editor state rather than a committed appearance.

One session serves the whole document, as the oracle holds one
`FPDF_FORMHANDLE` for one document: what page 0's replay leaves focused is
what page 1's replay starts from. The session is opened from the *same bytes
and same password* as the render path's document, via `Arc<[u8]>` — a session
over a different reading of the file would route clicks against annotations
the renderer never saw.

`Sent N events.` is unchanged and stays oracle-compatible: it counts the
**script's** lines, which is a property of the file, not of how many times the
stream was dispatched or of how many lines dispatch could use.

### What the facade still lacks for the renderer

This is the one thing the slice could not finish inside its write scope, and
it is recorded rather than worked around.

`render::render` now takes the `Vec<AppearanceUpdate>` the replay produced and
carries it to the point of use, but **cannot apply it**. The blocker is
precise:

1. **There is no seam to inject an appearance through.**
   `pdfrum_doc::annot_render::overlay` — the tool's `FPDF_ANNOT` pass, and the
   only place widget appearances reach the page graph — builds its own
   `AnnotOverlay` internally by calling `ap::generate_appearances_with_text`
   over the document. It takes no caller-supplied overlay and offers no
   override hook. Honouring `UpdateKind::Regenerated`/`LiveEdit` means giving
   `overlay` an `Option<&AnnotOverlay>` (or a pre-seeded one) that it merges
   *over* what it generates — a `pdfrum-doc` signature change this slice does
   not own. `AnnotOverlay::set` already exists and is exactly the right
   vehicle, so the change is small; it is simply not mine to make.

2. **The index space of `AnnotId` is not stated to be the overlay's.**
   `AnnotOverlay` is keyed by the **raw `/Annots` array index** — `overlay`
   looks up `list.source_indices[slot]`, not `slot`, and the two differ
   whenever a page has a pop-up, which `AnnotList::load` filters out.
   `session::AnnotId`'s doc says "in load order", which reads as the *filtered*
   index. Until one of the two says which it is, an update cannot be applied to
   the right annotation on any page carrying a pop-up. **This wants one
   sentence on `AnnotId::index`** naming the raw `/Annots` index (or a
   conversion where they differ). Cheap to settle now, silently wrong to guess.

3. **Nothing is blocked on the update *type* itself.** `UpdateKind::appearance()`
   already yields the `&GeneratedAp` that `ap::stream_dict` turns into the same
   form XObject `overlay` builds for a file-supplied `/AP`, so once (1) and (2)
   are settled the renderer needs no new data. `UpdateKind::Cleared` maps to
   "draw nothing generated", which `overlay` expresses by having no entry —
   though a *positive* cleared marker would be needed to suppress a file's own
   `/AP`, and the current enum cannot say that. Worth confirming when routing
   lands; it does not arise while dispatch is inert.

Until then `render` carries a `debug_assert!` that no update holding an
appearance ever reaches it. That is not a stub: it is the alarm that fires, in
tests and in the debug binary, on the first run where dispatch stops being
inert and this section stops being true.

## Two contracts the dispatch wiring forced into the open

Both were raised by the `--send-events` agent (`39336fa`) and both were
genuine ambiguities rather than bugs: the code did the right thing, but the
documented contract admitted a second reading that would have been silently
wrong. Both are now normative in SPEC §15.

### `AnnotId::index` is the **raw** `/Annots` index, pop-ups counted

The doc said "in load order", which reads as the pop-up-filtered position.
`pdfrum-doc`'s `AnnotList` drops pop-ups — they are synthesized separately —
so a widget's slot in that list is not its index in the file's array. The
appearance overlay the renderer applies updates through is keyed by
`source_indices[slot]`, the **raw** index. The two agree on every page without
a pop-up, which is what made the wrong reading dangerous: it would have
applied every appearance after the first pop-up to the wrong annotation.

Raw is the natural choice, not a concession — hit testing walks `/Annots`
itself, so it is the index the walk already holds. Verified: this crate
carries `AnnotId` opaquely and never derives an index, so no code changed;
three tests now pin it (a widget at raw index 1 behind a pop-up, the band sort
reordering without renumbering, and the focus ring keeping the gaps that
unfocusable annotations leave).

### `Cleared` → `RevertedToFileAppearance`

It mapped to "no overlay entry", and absence from the overlay already means
"use whatever the file declares" — so the variant could only ever have meant
*revert*, never *suppress*. The name now says so.

Suppression is not merely unimplemented, it is unneeded: `ResetAppearance`
always writes an appearance rather than blanking one, and the widgets that
genuinely draw nothing (signature, and types the classifier cannot name) never
receive interaction state, so they never produce an update at all. If a later
milestone needs real suppression it needs a new variant **and** a positive
marker in the overlay — the doc comment says so, so it is not spelled by
re-reading this one.

## Block 2: routing, and the two seams the pixels then asked for

### What was actually wrong (and the correction above)

The milestone's status said the pixel path was "end to end" and what remained
was "fidelity". The delivery half was indeed complete — `overlay_with`, the
raw-index-keyed `AnnotOverlay`, the tool's `session_overlay` and
`render::render` all worked. But **nothing was delivered through it**: the
facade's `dispatch` and `dispatch_keyboard` returned `Response::ignored()`

was ever typed. See the withdrawal under "The 18, by residue".

### What landed

- **`page.rs`** — the walk that was missing between the machines and the
  events. A page's raw `/Annots` array into `Candidate`s for the hit test,
  `Focusable`s for the tab ring, and per-widget configuration for a field's
  state. Deliberately not `pdfrum-doc`'s `AnnotList`, which drops pop-ups and

`scrollable_widgets2`). The 12-versus-14 rows come from
`crates/pdfrum-form/src/route.rs:741-746`: `row_height` returns the `/DA`
font size (12.0) where `CPWL_ListCtrl::Item::GetItemHeight`
(`cpwl_list_ctrl.cpp:49-51`) returns the laid-out
`GetContentRect().Height()` (13.392). The fix is to make `row_height` the
laid-out height — `vt::layout(label, &config, &metrics)
.content_rect_pdf(plate).height()`, the same call `ap::field_body::list_box`
makes per row — so `top_visible_for`, the wheel's visible-row count and the
hit test move together. No `pdfrum-doc` change; nothing was committed for
the metric. The caret and glyph-offset measurements above stand as
measurements; their attribution to the font tables does not.

### A divergence the port found, in `pdfrum-doc`'s selection reader

`/I [1 3]` on a field with no `/V` should select rows 1 and 3. It selects
**nothing**.

`CPDF_FormField::IsItemSelected` (`cpdf_formfield.cpp:546-554`) consults
**`/I` first**, as integer indices, and falls back to `/V` only when `/I` is
not *usable*. "Usable" is `UseSelectedIndicesObject` (`:863-935`): either
there is no `/V` at all, or `/I` has the same number of entries as `/V` and
every index names an option whose value appears in `/V`.

`ap::field_body::selected_indices` inverts both halves — it reads `/V` first,
consults `/I` only when `/V` is absent, and then compares each `/I` entry as
**text** against the option values, so an integer index matches no option.

The two readings agree on two of the three fixture fields, which is what let
the difference hide: with `/V` alone they agree trivially, and on
`Listbox_MultiSelectMultipleMismatch` (`/V` two entries against `/I` three)
upstream's consistency check *rejects* `/I` and falls back to `/V`, which is
the same answer. They differ on exactly one shape: **`/I` present, `/V`
absent.**

`crates/pdfrum/tests/form_listbox.rs::an_index_array_alone_currently_selects_nothing`
asserts the current answer with the rule in its doc comment, so correcting the
reader turns that test red rather than leaving it silently green. **The reader
is `pdfrum-doc`'s**, so this is recorded for that slice rather than fixed
here.

### What the port found that the corpus could not

Four defects, none visible in any `.evt` fixture, all caught by assertions
rather than by pixels. Worth stating together because they are the argument
for porting against the facade rather than against the edit control:

1. **Every click placed the caret at the same position.** The router passed a
   page-space point to a layout query that wants the appearance stream's
   space, so the hit test clamped to one end every time.
2. **A drag's release leg was dropped.** `mouse_up` cleared the anchor before
   extending the selection to the release point — and a drag whose only move
   *is* the release is exactly what upstream's `SelectTextWithMouse` sends.
3. **Tab could not reach the focus ring.** Keyboard dispatch refused every
   event without a focused field, and a Tab with nothing focused is what takes
   focus.
4. **A single-line field's text outran its layout.** The layout drops line
   breaks and the control stored the caller's string verbatim, so `"Foo\nBar"`
   reported seven characters against a layout holding six.
5. **An editable combo box reported no text.** `ChoiceState::focused_text`
   always answered with an option's label, and an editable box's text half is
   not an option.
6. **A focused link's Return did nothing.** `UpdateKind::ActionRequested` was
   declared and never constructed, so the one variant that exists to hand a
   caller a decision was unreachable.

The corpus renders none of these: its scripts click at a field's start, type
without clicking mid-text, and reach fields by clicking rather than tabbing.

#### Finding 1, and why the facade port could not see it

`focus_ring` called `FocusRing::build(&focusables, TabOrder::Structure)`
unconditionally, so `TabOrder::from_tabs` — written, tested and correct — was
unreachable from the live path. `annotiter.pdf` exists to catch exactly this:
three pages listing the same four widgets under `/Tabs /R`, `/C` and `/S`.
`FormFillFirstTab` expects annot **1**; structure order answers **0**.

The port could not see it because `form_tab.rs` asserted only
`focused_annot().is_some()`. It now asserts the upstream numbers: first Tab is
annot 1, first Shift+Tab is annot 0, the walks are `1,2,3,0` and `0,3,2,1`,
and a new test asserts the three pages disagree — `1,2,3,0` / `1,3,2,0` /
`0,1,2,3`.

**Those three orders were not taken on trust from either side.** The column
pass's seed rules are not readable from the source alone (M14.md records two
of them as upstream quirks), so `CPDFSDK_AnnotIterator::GenerateResults` was
transcribed verbatim into C++, compiled, and run against `annotiter.pdf`'s own
four rectangles. It prints `Row [1,2,3,0]`, `Column [1,3,2,0]`,
`Structure [0,1,2,3]` — matching this crate exactly, the `fLeft < 0` seed
quirk included.

`/Tabs` is read off the page dictionary **directly**, not inherited:
`GetTabOrder` (`cpdfsdk_annotiterator.cpp:111-122`) calls `GetByteStringFor`
on the page's own dict, so a `/Tabs` on `/Pages` reaches no page.

#### Finding 2, and the reader that is *not* wrong

There are two readers of a list box's selection upstream and **they disagree
on purpose**, so this was never one reader with a bug in it.

- The **appearance** producer, for a file with no `/NeedAppearances`, is
  `CPDFSDK_AppStream::SetAsListBox` → `GetSelectedIndex` →
  `GetValueOrSelectedIndicesObject`: `/V` first, matched as text.
  `ap::field_body::selected_indices` reproduces that and **stays as it is**.
- The **interaction** reader is `CPDF_FormField::IsItemSelected`
  (`cpdf_formfield.cpp:546-554`): `/I` first, as integer indices, falling back
  to `/V` only when `/I` is not *usable* — `UseSelectedIndicesObject`
  (`:863-935`). `FORM_IsIndexSelected` is that function, and a session's
  `ChoiceState` must be seeded from it.

They agree except on one shape — `/I` present, `/V` absent — where the first
selects nothing and the second selects the rows `/I` names.
`listbox_form.pdf`'s indices field is exactly that shape, which is why the
divergence hid: the other two fixture fields agree trivially.

`pdfrum-doc` landed `form::selected_indices_for_interaction` (`a8d0962`);
`WidgetInfo::selected` calls it. The pinning test flipped from "selects
nothing" to rows `[1, 3]` **in the same commit**, so the tree never held a red
step, and `CheckIfMultipleSelectedValues` was tightened from "at least two
rows" to its own indices `[2, 4]` at the same time.

#### Findings 3 and 4 — one space, and the fix the review proposed would have
#### been wrong

`row_at` subtracted a **page-space** event point from an
**appearance-space** rectangle. `ap::widget::rotated_rect` puts a widget's box
at the origin, so for `listbox_form.pdf`'s single-select box at
`/Rect [100 350 200 380]` the arithmetic is `29 - 371`: a large negative
quotient, a saturating `usize`, and no row at all.

It was invisible because the click still *focused* the widget —
`mouse_down` focuses before `choice_click` — and the only test asserted
`rows.len() == 1`, which the file's pre-existing selection satisfied for free.
The tests now assert the upstream Y constants land on the upstream rows: 371
selects row 0, 358 selects row 1.

On finding 4 the review's diagnosis was right and **its proposed fix would
have moved every caret a line**. It suggested routing through
`geom::Plate::to_plate`, which applies the rotation inverse *and* a y-down
flip; `vt::hit`'s queries flip y themselves, so that double-flips. What was
actually missing is the y-**up** half — `CFFL_FormField::FFLtoPWL`, the
inverse of `GetCurMatrix` (`cffl_formfield.cpp:442-464`). That is now
`Plate::to_widget`, and `to_plate` is built on top of it, the two differing
only by the flip.

The four-quadrant table was verified rather than transcribed: `GetCurMatrix`
was compiled verbatim, inverted the way `FFLtoPWL` inverts it, and run over
the widget's four corners. Sixteen points, four rotations, exact match.

The rotation itself is read off `/MK /R` with the same truncating `% 360` that
`rotated_rect` and `CPDFSDK_Widget::GetRotate` (`cpdfsdk_widget.cpp:458-461`)
use — **not** `Rotation::from_degrees`'s `rem_euclid` — because routing and the
generator must agree about which box a click lands in. A `/R -90` is upright to
both.

#### Findings 5, 6, 9, 11 — four rules, one mistake

All four are the same error in different places: reaching for a generic answer
where a subclass overrides it, or dropping an argument the oracle passes on.

- **The focus box.** A combo box returns an empty rectangle
  *unconditionally* (`cpwl_combo_box.cpp:321-323`) — the override takes no
  branch, so editable and gated are one row, not two; we answered `None` only
  when editable and inflated the window otherwise. A push button **deflates**
  by its border (`cpwl_special_button.cpp:21-24`) where the generic answer
  inflates by one: the opposite sign on the same number, and it had been
  folded into the toggle arm. The whole table is now written beside the match,
  row by row against the `GetFocusRect` that produces it.
- **The wheel.** `CPWL_ListBox::OnMouseWheel` (`cpwl_list_box.cpp:357-368`)
  hands `IsSHIFTKeyDown`/`IsCTRLKeyDown` to the same `OnVK_DOWN`/`OnVK_UP` the
  arrow keys call. `OnVK` (`cpwl_list_ctrl.cpp:242-265`) branches three ways,
  and the first is the surprising one: **Ctrl's body is empty** — only the
  caret moves. That reads as an oversight and is not; it is how a caret is
  walked to a row before the row is toggled, and it is why a multi-select list
  reports its focused text from the caret. The arrow keys get the same
  treatment, because `OnKeyDown` (`cpwl_list_box.cpp:103-119`) passes the
  flags too and the two gestures are one operation upstream. A combo box now
  consumes the wheel without moving: it has no `OnMouseWheel`, so it falls to
  `CPWL_Wnd`'s, which returns false unless a child holds the keyboard capture
  — and a closed combo's list is not shown.
- **The double click.** `CPWL_Edit::OnLButtonDblClk` (`cpwl_edit.cpp:636-644`)
  calls `SelectAll()`. The embeddertest's comment says "the entire line" and
  its field is single-line, so the two agree there and only there. The ported
  test called `select_line_at` **directly**, so it tested the wrong operation
  and could never have caught it; it now drives `select_all`, and a second
  test builds a three-line field and asserts the two readings actually diverge
  before pinning the one the oracle takes.
- **`clear_siblings`.** `CPDF_FormField::CheckControl`
  (`cpdf_formfield.cpp:683-716`) sets the clicked control to its on state and
  every sibling to `Off`. One shared `ToggleState` cannot hold a per-control
  `/AS`, but it can hold **which** control is the chosen one, and now does
  (`ToggleState::checked_control`). Drawing the difference needs an
  appearance-state override that `ap::widget::generate_with_live` does not
  offer — it takes a `LiveState` for a body's text and a `Highlight` for a
  caret, and nothing for `/AS`. That gap is named in the function's doc rather
  than faked, and recording the chosen control now makes it a one-line change
  when the seam exists.

#### Finding 7 — the public entry point had its own implementation

`force_kill_focus` reimplemented dropping focus and got it half right: it
returned a `FocusChanged` update and **no appearance**. Dropping focus is what
turns a field's live editor state back into a generated stream (brief §3.3
step 6), so the field it dropped kept drawing its caret and its uncommitted
text. A left click that misses already went through `route::kill_focus`, which
does the redraw and the undo clear; the public analogue did not.

`route::kill_focus` is now public and is the only implementation. The facade
grew one helper, `with_page`, so a routing `Context` is assembled in exactly
one place.

**The doctest is what had been hiding it**: it asserted the response was
non-empty, which a bare `FocusChanged` satisfies. It now asserts an appearance
came back, which is the property that was broken.

### The pixel bug: a list row is the laid-out line, not the font size

`route::row_height` returned the `/DA` font size where
`CPWL_ListCtrl::Item::GetItemHeight` (`cpwl_list_ctrl.cpp:49-51`) returns the
item edit's `GetContentRect().Height()` — the laid-out line, which is
`(ascent - descent) * size / 1000`. At 12 points in Arimo that is **13.392**,
not 12, so every row was an eighth short and the shortfall accumulated down
the box. `ReArrange` (`:525-551`) stacks the items by exactly that height,
which is why one number moved the scroll clamp, the wheel's visible-row count
and the hit test together.

It is now measured with the **same call** `ap::field_body::list_box` makes per
row — `vt::layout` into a zero-height plate, so the layout reports the row's
own extent rather than the box's. That is the point: an earlier attempt to
correct one of the two alone was reverted for making the pixels *worse*
(1877 → 1916 differing), because the height that is drawn is `pdfrum-doc`'s
and the height that is hit-tested is this crate's, and changing one
desynchronises them.

Because the height now depends on an option's label, `row_height` and
`visible_rows` take the `ChoiceState`; the two call sites read the state before
taking their mutable borrow.

**The test asserts the property, not the constant**, and the reason is worth
recording: the constant is a *face's*, and the test binary does not load the
corpus's. Without `--croscore-font-names` the fallback face gives a laid-out
row of **11.24** units at 12 points, not Arimo's 13.392. So the test walks
down the widget, finds the row edges (measured at y 367.75 and 356.51), and
asserts they are evenly spaced by a step that is measurably **not** 12.

### The RTL selection band, and the two defects that are not ours

`form_textfield_selected_rtl` was the row the `pdfrum-doc` owner's selection
fix (F2) was held back for. Three defects were found; **one was this crate's**
and is fixed, and the other two are named with their C++ citations rather than
guessed at.

#### Ours, and fixed: the band collapsed on a right-to-left line

`selection_bands` spanned one rectangle from the caret at the selection's
start to the caret at its end, per line. That is right for a left-to-right
line and assumes something only a left-to-right line has: that the carets
advance monotonically with the word index.

They do not. On a right-to-left line the layout places the first word at the
line's right edge and the last at its left, so for the fixture's Hebrew the
carets run **1, 19, 13, 7** — the two endpoints are the run's *interior*.
Measured, the band came out **six units wide where the oracle paints fifty**,
about one character of ten.

`CPWL_EditImpl::DrawEdit` (`cpwl_edit_impl.cpp:659-676`) has no such
assumption: it walks the selected words and fills `GetWordRect(word, line)` —
`[x, x + width]` at the line's ascent and descent — once per word. That is
direction-agnostic by construction, which is why upstream needs no
right-to-left case at all. Reproduced here from the two carets that bound each
word, merging rectangles that touch so a contiguous run stays one fill.

A **section break is skipped** rather than filled. It holds an index in the
flat numbering, because the tokenizer counted it, but it is not a word and
`DrawEdit`'s loop fills only what `GetWord` returns. Filling it produced a
rectangle spanning from one line's end to the next line's start — covering
both lines whole. A two-line test pins that.

#### Not ours: `vt::hit::caret_rect` ignores `is_rtl`, in two places

Recorded for the `pdfrum-doc` slice. The C++ spells both rules out:

- `CPVT_Word::CaretX()` (`cpvt_word.h:42`) is
  `is_rtl ? location.x : location.x + width`. Ours always adds the width, so
  the caret after a right-to-left character lands one advance right of where
  it belongs.
- `CPVT_VariableText::Iterator::GetLineCaretX`
  (`cpvt_variabletext.cpp:103-118`) returns `line.x + line.width` when a
  line's **first** word is rtl, and `line.x` otherwise. Ours always returns
  the left edge, so a right-to-left line's header caret is on the wrong side
  of the line entirely.

Measured on `בחר` at 12 points in a plate spanning x 1..99: our carets are
`1, 19, 13, 7` where they should be `19, 13, 7, 1`. Both wrong values follow
from the one missing branch.

The band fix does not depend on it: with the carets as they are, the per-word
bands' union already spans the correct extent (1→61 against the old 1→7), and
it becomes exact when the caret rule lands.

#### Not ours: the glyphs are a font-encoding fallback

The analysis attributed "Latin mojibake where Hebrew belongs" to this crate's
live-edit path. **It is not there.** Driving the session with the `.evt`'s own
ten `charcode` lines and reading back its text gives
`[1489, 1495, 1512, 46, 46, 46, 32, 1488, 1490, 1514]` — `בחר... אגת`, exactly
right — and a double click selects all ten. The `to_char` → `on_char` →
`TextEdit` path is clean end to end.

The substitution happens when the appearance stream is written. The fixture's
font is a bare `/Type /Font /Subtype /TrueType /BaseFont /Arial` with no
`/Encoding` and no `/Widths`, and `ap::TextFont::encode` falls through to
`unmapped_code` for U+05D0..U+05EA, emitting a Latin byte. That is
`pdfrum-doc`'s encoder.

## Block 4: two font substitutions over one document, and a scroll that was never ported

Three defects, two of them the same defect wearing different clothes. The
form-events cluster goes from **15 failures to 5**, and the board from 1641 to
**1651 pass** with the other 1675 rows byte-identical.

### The mechanism, in one sentence

**A form session measured its appearances with a different font substitution
from the one the page beside it was drawn with**, and separately, the typed
path never reached the second face the stored path already had.

### 1 — The session's fonts were not the render path's (`4a25a37`)

`FormSession::build` made its own `pdfrum_page::BuildContext::new()`. That
context carries **default** substitution options — no `font_dirs`, no Croscore
rename — while `pdfrum-tool` renders through
`BuildContext::with_substitution(substitution_options(options))`
(`run.rs:302`). One document, two substitutions.

On the hermetic corpus this is not a subtlety. PLAN.md §4's determinism recipe
is `--font-dir=third_party/test_fonts --croscore-font-names`, and
`third_party/test_fonts` carries no Arial — it carries the metric-compatible
**Arimo**, at 905 up and 211 down. The default context finds no directory to
enumerate, so `/Arial` falls through to the **built-in base-14 Helvetica** at
718 and −219.

| metrics | caret height at 12pt | form-space `Td` y |
|---|---:|---:|
| base-14 Helvetica 718/−219 | 11.244 | 12.006 (what we emitted) |
| Arimo 905/−211 | **13.392** | 10.836 (the oracle's) |

The 1.17-unit difference is one device row at 1:1, and it is the one-row
offset M14-doc §8.5 measured on both LTR rows.

**`vt::hit::caret_rect` was never the defect.** Handed the `FormFonts` face
under the hermetic options it already answered device rows 38.304–51.696 — the
oracle's exactly. The query was right and the metrics reaching it were wrong.
`docs/status/pdfrum-render.md` records this class of error already ("the
metrics the layout stacks lines by are the substituted face's"); the session
was the one caller that never got the fix.

**The shape.** `FormSession::with_context` and `with_config_in` take the
caller's `BuildContext`, the way `Page::render_with` and `Page::text_with`
already do; `new` and `with_config` keep a default one, which is right for a
caller that renders through a default one too — the facade's own `Page::render`
does. `SubstitutionOptions` is re-exported so a caller can build one without
reaching for `pdfrum-font`. `pdfrum-tool` now makes its context once per file,
**before** the session, and hands the same one to both; it was already making
exactly this context for the page walk.

Recorded in SPEC §15.8 by `19f5e10`, because the constructor pair is new
surface and the reason it exists is a contract rather than a convenience.

Tests: `crates/pdfrum/tests/form_substitution.rs`. The fixture is
`form_textfield_focused_ltr.in` expanded, vendored as
`substituted_da_font.pdf` with its provenance recorded — nothing embedded, so
which face lays the field out is entirely the substitution's choice. The
assertion is the two numbers above: **13.392 under the hermetic options,
11.244 under the default**, parsed literally out of the emitted `re`.

### 2 — The typed path never reached the second face (`8e45897`)

`route.rs::generate` called `ap::widget::generate_with_live`, which forwards no
substitute, so a field being **typed into** wrote the low byte of each code
point through its `/DA` font. Hebrew came out as Latin mojibake, where the same
value *stored* in `/V` had reached the second face since `pdfrum-doc`'s
`266783f`.

`CPDF_BAFontMap::GetWordFontIndex` (`core/fpdfdoc/cpdf_bafontmap.cpp:116-151`)
asks its charset question **per character** and knows nothing about whether the
character was typed or stored, so the typed path takes the same answer.
`pdfrum-doc` landed `generate_with_live_faces` and its `LiveInput` record for
exactly this migration (`6542afb`), naming this call site in its doc comment;
this commit is the other half.

**The half that is easy to get wrong is the width closure.** `with_font` now
builds the substitute the way `ap::generate_appearances_with_text` builds it
for the stored path, and the substitute enters through the **widths**, not only
through the encoder. A run set in two faces advances by two faces' metrics;
measuring it all with the first gives a line the wrong length wherever the
second writes. That is precisely how `form_textfield_selected_rtl`'s selection
band came to end at device column 101 while its glyphs ran to 111 — ten columns
of dark where the oracle's are white, and the residue that held **F2** back at
−0.00095.

Every `with_font` caller gets the corrected metrics, not just the generator:
the edit's initial layout, the wheel's content height, and `highlight_of`'s
caret and band rectangles all measure through the same closure now.

**F2's hold is clear.** The two RTL rows were the block's worst at 0.947; they
are now **0.997558** and **0.999385**. The band and the glyphs agree because
they are measured with the same two faces they are set in.

### 3 — `ScrollToCaret` had no port at all (`e451d7f`)

The measurement pass read `password` as "scrolled one character short". It is
not scrolled at all: `TextEdit::scroll.0` was written **nowhere in the tree**.
`ScrollToCaret` (`fpdfsdk/pwl/cpwl_edit_impl.cpp:1246-1286`) runs after
essentially every mutation and every caret move upstream, and
`docs/design/pdfrum-form.md:1658` lists it in the ported postlude sequence that
never got written.

**The port is two coordinate frames.** Upstream keeps `scroll_pos_point_.x` as
an absolute layout position seeded at `rcPlate.left`, and `VTToEdit`
(`:1105-1106`) converts by subtracting `scroll_pos_point_.x - rcPlate.left`. We
store a **distance** — `LiveState::shift` negates it — so ours is upstream's
minus that term, and every branch drops it:

| upstream | here |
|---|---|
| `VTToEdit(head).x` | `head - scroll.0` |
| `SetScrollPosX(ptHead.x)` | `scroll.0 = head - plate.left` |
| `SetScrollPosX(ptHead.x - rcPlate.Width())` | `scroll.0 = head - plate.left - width` |

**The asymmetry is load-bearing and is kept:** the comparisons are made on the
**edit-space** point and the assignment from the **layout-space** one. Reading
both in one frame is the obvious simplification and is wrong by exactly one
advance. The three comparisons carry `FXSYS_IsFloatSmaller`/`IsFloatEqual`'s
0.0001 tolerance (`core/fxcrt/fx_system.h:36-41`), so a caret landing exactly
on the edge counts as inside; `SetScrollLimit` (`:1207-1234`) runs first, which
is what pins a short value flush left.

`enable_scroll_` (`cpwl_edit_impl.h:284`) becomes `TextEdit::auto_scroll`, set
from the `DoNotScroll` flag `field/mod.rs:81` had parsed since block 1 and
nothing had ever read. `CFFL_TextField::GetCreateParam`
(`cffl_textfield.cpp:54-63`) raises `kEditAutoScroll` for any text field
without it, multi-line or not.

Only the horizontal half is ported. Upstream's vertical branches carry doubled
conditions against thrashing when the caret is taller than the plate, and
nothing in the corpus reaches them.

Five tests in `crates/pdfrum-form/tests/scroll_to_caret.rs`, on a ten-character
plate with a one-unit-per-character face. The one worth naming is
`the_scroll_is_a_distance_and_not_an_absolute_position`: it puts the same field
at `x = 0` and at `x = 100` and requires the same answer, which is the
assertion a port that kept upstream's absolute position fails.

### Two rulings, recorded as documented residues

Both from the orchestrator, both deliberately **not** attempted here.

**(i) LCD subpixel antialiasing on live-edit text — real oracle behaviour, out
of scope for M14.** `DrawTextString` (`fpdfsdk/pwl/cpwl_edit_impl.cpp:51-56`)
builds a **local** `CPDF_RenderOptions` and never lets the flag word touch it.
Its constructor (`core/fpdfapi/render/cpdf_renderoptions.cpp:23-27`) sets
`options_.bClearType = true` — overwriting the header's `= false` — and the
call site `CHECK`s it. The two **public** render entry points clear it from the
flag word (`cpdfsdk_renderpage.cpp:37`, `fpdf_formfill.cpp:272`, both
`!!(flags & FPDF_LCD_TEXT)`), and `pdfium_test` never passes `FPDF_LCD_TEXT` —
**but `DrawTextString` is reached from neither.** So the text a live edit draws
is the only text on the page rendered with ClearType on: `aliasing_type`
becomes `kLcd` (`cpdf_textrenderer.cpp:36-40`) and the oracle's glyphs carry
colour fringes ours cannot. **291 px on `focused_ltr`, 447 on `selected_ltr`**
at the time of measurement; after this block `focused_ltr`'s residue is 319
differing pixels of which **291 (91%) are exactly this**, so it is now the
whole of that row. Ruled **out of scope for M14** — it needs a per-draw
text-antialiasing selection in `crates/pdfrum-render`, where `RenderOptions::
text_aa` is whole-render today. **Reopening condition:** a consumer needing
`FFLDraw`-exact fringes.

**(ii) The widget tint one count low — owed to the raster crates, not M14.**
The tint composites to `(240, 244, 255)` where the oracle has
`(241, 244, 255)`: one count on the **red** channel alone, over **2440 px** on
`password` (2482 measured after this block). The colour itself is right and is
pinned by `annot_render`'s own
`the_highlight_colour_is_bgr_and_composites_to_the_goldens_tint` — `0xFFE4DD`
read as BGR is `(0xDD, 0xE4, 0xFF)` at alpha 100/255, and upstream's truncating
merge `((255 * (255 - a)) + c * a) / 255` gives 241. Recomputed three ways:
truncation gives 241, rounding 242, the float product 241.667. We render
**240**, lower than all three, so the loss is the backend's own alpha
compositing rounding. Filed as **a small item against the raster crates**, not
M14's.
