# M21 — Comparative benchmarks, first pass

**Contract:** PLAN.md §M21. **Method and tables:** `docs/benchmarks/README.md`.
**Script:** `benches/compare/`. **Data:** `docs/benchmarks/data/`.
**Started:** 2026-09-05. This is the first pass: the harness, first numbers
on two corpora, the adoption table, a parallel-render throughput row, and
the coverage matrix. Nothing in the library crates or the CLI was touched.

## What was built

`benches/compare` — its own cargo workspace, severed from the root one the
way `fuzz/` is, so the peers never enter the tree `scripts/ci.nu`'s
pure-Rust check walks. Verified: `cargo tree -e normal --workspace` at the
root still names no peer and no `-sys`/`cc`/`cmake`/`pkg-config`/`bindgen`
crate beyond the three exemptions; the root `cargo deny check` is clean and
the root `deny.toml` is untouched. `benches/compare/deny.toml` carries the
root allowlist plus the benchmark-only exceptions (AGPL-3.0 for
`mupdf`/`mupdf-sys`; the `ttf-parser` unmaintained advisory reached only
through `pdf_oxide` and `pdf-extract`; the `cc`/`bindgen` ban lifted because
measuring `mupdf` means compiling it) with a comment on each.

Four subcommands: `run` (one child process per engine × file × operation,
10 s deadline, cold + warm timings, `VmHWM`, correctness against
`pdfium_test`'s PNG through the conformance SSIM and against its UTF-32 text
exact / whitespace-normalized / token F1, one JSON, resumable), `report`
(every table from the JSON alone), `adoption` (a minimal consumer crate per
engine: crates in the tree, native build crates, clean build time, stripped
size, `unsafe` count, licence) and `throughput` (every page of every file on
N threads, each engine in its own multi-threading model — a shared `Sync`
document, MuPDF's shared display lists, or PDFium's one thread — pages per
second).

Engines: `pdfrum` (the facade, default features), `hayro` 0.7.1,
`hayro-interpret` 0.7.0 (text assembled by the harness from its glyph
stream, labelled as such), `pdf-extract` 0.12.0, `lopdf` 0.44.0, `pdf` 0.10.0,
`pdf_oxide` 0.3.77 (with its `rendering` feature), `pdfium-render` 0.9.3
bound to the pypdfium2 `libpdfium.so` MinerU installed, `mupdf` 0.8.0
compiled from its vendored source with `default-features = false` +
`base14-fonts`. `pdf-render` 1.0-beta (PLAN.md's second render peer) is a
commercial-licence fork of hayro and was not measured.

## Deviations from the brief, all deliberate

- **Sample size.** "200 files, every 8th of `testing/corpus`" is not one
  rule: the directory holds 836 PDFs, so every 8th is 105. Every 4th (209
  files, sorted recursive listing from index 0) is what ran, and the rule is
  in the JSON.
- **`lopdf` text.** The brief said open + object walk only; `lopdf` has an
  `extract_text` and the row is more informative with it. Both are reported.
- **Passwords.** Every PDFium encryption fixture needs a user password, so
  the coverage spec carries one per file and the harness hands it to the
  oracle and to every engine's password API. Two of the five (R2 and R6)
  are PDFium's own "âge" test password, passed as UTF-8 to the engines that
  take `&str`.
- **The box was not idle.** Load averages between 18 and 88 on 32 cores
  during the runs, from other agents' builds; every run's header line
  records the `uptime` before and after. Ratios between engines measured
  back to back are the reliable reading; absolute milliseconds are noisier
  than `benches/baseline.json`'s.
- **Warm loop.** A warm rerun that would not fit the 6 s budget is skipped
  rather than started, after the first corpus run produced a "timeout" for
  pdfrum on `image_bug_583804.pdf` that was the harness repeating a 2 s
  render three times, not the engine hanging.

## Where pdfrum stands (the tables have the numbers)

**Correctness.** On both corpora pdfrum is the closest engine to the
oracle among the Rust ones, and on PDFium's own corpus it is closer than
`pdfium-render` (a different PDFium build with system fonts): render median
SSIM 0.9995 / 0.9992, 35/44 and 180/209 pages inside the conformance floor;
text whitespace-normalized on 39/44 and 198/208 pages. It opens 252 of the
253 files across both runs; the one it does not is public-key encryption
(`Adobe.PubSec`), which it reports as unsupported. No panic, no crash, no
timeout in 759 child runs. The peers' robustness is the informative half of
those tables: `pdf-extract` panics on 29 files (an `unsupported encoding`
`panic!`), `pdf-rs` cannot open a third of either corpus, `lopdf` and
`pdf-extract` refuse 46 of the 209 FRC files with damaged trailers that
every other engine recovers, and `pdf_oxide`'s renderer errors on 40 of 209.

**Speed — a loss, published.** pdfrum is the slowest renderer at the
median on both corpora: warm 15.9 ms / 10.4 ms against `pdfium-render`'s
8.3 / 4.1, mupdf's 6.1 / 3.4 and hayro's 15.0 / 5.4. Cold (first render of
a fresh session) it is 76 ms / 42 ms, three to nine times the others, and
the throughput table shows the same thing from the batch side: 39 pages/s
single-threaded on the multi-page files against hayro's 70, mupdf's 149 and
`pdfium-render`'s 153, scaling 1.9× to four threads and flat after. Of the
C engines only PDFium is genuinely single-threaded (one global state, every
call on one thread); mupdf threads in MuPDF's own model — serial
display-list record, parallel rasterization — and reaches 220 pages/s at
four threads, scaling 1.5× against pdfrum's 1.9× because its serial record
loop is 17.7 % of the timed pass. Open and
text are competitive (text warm median 0.58 ms / 0.07 ms; open 0.18 /
0.03 ms). The cold-render and per-session cost — font enumeration in every
new `RenderSession` — is the first work item this milestone produces.

**Memory — a loss.** Peak RSS on `image_bug_583804.pdf` is 1.5 GiB where
every peer stays under 1 GiB; median render memory (52 MiB on the 44-file
corpus, 44 MiB on the sample) is in the pack with hayro and the C engines.

**Losses by file.** 16 on the 44-file corpus, 17 on the sample, listed in
the README and in `compare report --losses`. Render: `vector_en_system.pdf`
(0.960 where every peer is above 0.98), `vector_tcpdf_009.pdf` /
`third_party/tcpdf/example_009.pdf` (0.959), `image_en_fqa.pdf` (0.977),
`image_jpx_123.pdf` (0.987), `shading_tcpdf_058.pdf`, `fx/image/1_image.pdf`,
`fx/path/transparent1.pdf`, `tcpdf/example_025.pdf`. Text:
`text_quick_start.pdf` (F1 0.641 — table-of-contents dot leaders broken
into lines where PDFium keeps them on one), `text_tcpdf_055.pdf`,
`text_bug_1029.pdf`, and the nine `FRC_8.2.4` pages where PDFium's text
carries a U+0003 between "Foxit" and "PhantomPDF" and pdfrum's does not
(oracle-bug rule: cited, not chased).

**Adoption.** 88 crates in `cargo add pdfrum`'s tree, no native build
crate, 16 s clean release build on this box, 1.1 MiB stripped hello-world,
`forbid(unsafe_code)` (the 4 counted are the word in comments), 6 932
`unsafe` in the dependency tree. `pdf_oxide` with rendering is 138 crates
and 54 s; `pdfium-render` is 25 crates and 16 704 generated `unsafe` around
a C++ library it does not build; `mupdf` is 12 crates, AGPL-3.0, and
compiles MuPDF with `cc` and `bindgen`.

## Coverage

`benches/compare/coverage.json` names one PDFium corpus file per feature
in the PLAN.md list; `data/2026-09-05-114527a71d4c-coverage.json` is the
run and the README's coverage matrix is the table. pdfrum opens, renders
and extracts every one of the 22 files, inside the floor on 18 renders;
the four outside are CCITT (`3bigpreview.pdf`, 0.880 — every engine is
between 0.84 and 0.88 on it: a bilevel scan every engine downsamples
differently from the oracle), JPX (0.987), and the two axial/radial shading fixtures at
0.988–0.989. Among the peers: hayro fails R2 and renders the Gouraud
type-4 mesh at 0.372 (mupdf too); pdf_oxide errors on R2, JPX, and the
function and radial shadings and renders types 5 and 6 at 0.74 / 0.65;
pdf-extract panics on the CCITT and CID files and cannot open R5, the five
`fx/shading` files, `js.pdf` or the incremental update; pdf-rs opens 12 of
22; lopdf extracts nothing from the R2, JBIG2 and Type3 files (F1 0.00).

## Gates

`cargo fmt --check` and `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings` on
`benches/compare` with both feature sets (the workspace lints, `unsafe`
forbidden, pedantic on); its 8 unit tests; `cargo deny check` for the
compare workspace against its own `deny.toml`; root `cargo fmt --all
--check`, root `cargo deny check` and the `scripts/ci.nu` pure-Rust name
check run by hand — all green. The root workspace was not built by this
milestone; nothing in it changed.
