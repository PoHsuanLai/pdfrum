# M12b — Second performance pass: resolution-aware decode, the walk, and the arena question

**Status: complete** (2026-09-01). This is the milestone-level record above four
item documents — `M12b-P1.md`, `M12b-P2.md`, `M12b-P3.md` and `M12c.md` — and it
is written in M12.md's register: hypothesis, A/B, verdict, **including the
non-results**. It does not restate those documents; it says what the milestone
set out to do, what measurement did to that intention, what it declined, and
what it owes. Read §1 and §7 if you read nothing else.

M12.md §10 and §11 are the model for two sections here. §10 exists because two
performance claims were withdrawn after an A/B refuted them; §11 is the
scorecard that scores every target against the number it is judged on rather
than against the average. This milestone needed both sections more than M12 did.

---

## 1. The result, stated first

**Three of this milestone's four items had their premise corrected by
measurement, and the corrections are worth more than the wins.**

- **P1** was aimed at scaled JPEG decode on the orchestrator's diagnosis that
  the oracle's `scale_denom` was the dominant image gap. Only **one** of the
  four documents PLAN.md cites as evidence is a JPEG at all. On that one file
  `zune-jpeg` is **17%** of the build and a `/Decode` array evaluated per byte
  over 100 M bytes is **83%**. The largest win in the item is downstream of
  every codec — deleting a float round trip *proved* to be the identity on all
  256 byte values. **The premise was wrong and the target was missed**
  (−33.4% on the `image` class against ≥40%).
- **P2** closed the `bumpalo` question **against** the arena, on an A/B showing
  **+265% / +121% / +97% slower** than a tuned no-dep baseline, plus a lifetime
  in a public type that disqualifies the design independently. Its lasting
  contribution is not the three changes that landed but the profile they were
  ranked from: **colour conversion ≤ 7.2%, allocation ~0 after reuse,
  interpretation 60–90% of the engine half.**
- **P3** was **retargeted mid-milestone** because P1 and P2 jointly retired its
  original premise, and then delivered the milestone's largest result:
  **−42.6%** on the engine half of `vector_paths_1751`, **−27.9%** on the whole
  render, confirmed independently by criterion on three rasterizer backends. It
  also *corrected P2 twice* — the "0.6% allocation" figure was a partial census
  and the real count was 21 421 allocations / 5.6 MiB, and `pattern.rs`, which
  P2 named as unowned residue, was **exonerated**: the cost is in
  `shading/patch.rs`, in no measurement bucket at all.
- **M12c** shipped a GPU backend that is **measurably correct and currently
  unusable by its intended consumer**. Zero Tier-C interior differences on 44 of
  44; 4.8x on the heaviest vector page. And vello 0.10 pins `wgpu` 29 while
  `egui-wgpu` needs `^30` and `iced_wgpu` `^27`, so no released frontend can
  inject a device. **The exemption's cost is paid; its benefit waits upstream.**

Two things run through all four and belong at the top rather than in a footnote:

**Measurement integrity is this milestone's real character** (§7). The machine
carried unrelated jobs at 300–455% CPU throughout, and the milestone produced
**four near-false findings that were caught rather than published**. Each is
recorded with its mechanism, because a future reader who sees a surprising
number on this machine needs to recognize the shape.

**The bench ratchet debt is unpaid** (§8), deliberately, and a non-zero exit is
not a verdict on the engineering: the warm family — the convention M12's exit
target is judged on — returned **132 entries, 32 improved, ZERO regressed**.
(`scripts/ratchet-decide.py` exits **2**, its "leave the update unwritten" code,
not 1; verified in §11.)

## 2. P1 — a decoder feature that turned out not to be the cost

*Detail: `docs/status/M12b-P1.md`. §1 reads the oracle, §4 has the measurement
that changed the question, §9 the targets.*

**The assignment's hypothesis:** the oracle computes a libjpeg `scale_denom`
from the destination size and decodes large images at reduced resolution inside
the inverse DCT; we decode at full resolution and throw the excess away. PLAN.md
names four documents as the evidence — `image_en_fqa`, `image_bug_718762`,
`image_bug_898443`, `image_bug_583804`.

**What measurement did to it, in two steps.** First, reading `cpdf_dib.cpp`
rather than assuming: the oracle passes its level count to exactly two branches,
JPX and DCT. Three of the four cited documents are **Flate**, which neither
implementation resolution-reduces. Second, on `image_bug_718762` — the one file
that *is* a JPEG — instrumenting the build put `zune_jpeg` at **17%** against
`apply_codec_decode` at **83%**: a `/Decode` array evaluated per byte over a
hundred million bytes, which is a kibibyte of lookup table.

So the item's deliverable is not a decoder feature. What landed:

| change | measured |
|---|---|
| The decode-target seam (SPEC.md §7 via `[spec]`) — `BuildContext.decode_target` as plain data, the cache's third lookup rung, resolution as part of cache identity | plumbing; correctness-shaped, not speed-shaped |
| JPX wired to `hayro-jpeg2000`'s `target_resolution` | **non-result on speed** — no corpus file is large enough relative to its page for the oracle's own rule to reduce it; it also removed a latent double-reduction bug that was unreachable only because nothing ever asked for a reduction |
| `/Decode` as a 1 KiB lookup table | **−67.9%** on `build/image_bug_718762` |
| `to_pixmap` without a float round trip proved to be the identity on all 256 byte values | **−35%** on the `image` class's cold render geomean |

**The finding that outlives the numbers**, and it is P1's real contribution:
the oracle's advantage on large images was never mainly its scaled decode — it
is that its per-sample path is cheap. Two changes downstream of every codec
moved the JBIG2 and Flate documents as much as the JPEG one.

**What was declined, on evidence rather than on time** (P1 §10): fusing the box
filter into the conversion, because `CStretchEngine` accumulates from an
already-converted scanline so the oracle converts every source pixel too;
sharpening the decode target to the per-image footprint, because the oracle
deliberately uses the whole page bitmap and the conservatism is what bounds the
error. Both would have changed pixels for a restructure the oracle does not
perform. The JPEG `scale_denom` itself is an upstream change and is written up
with our numbers — *including the one that argues against its urgency* — at
`docs/upstream/zune-jpeg-scaled-decode.md`.

Conformance was **byte-identical after all three commits**, and two of the three
code changes are *proved* identical by exhaustive test rather than observed
identical by measurement.

## 3. P2 — a dependency question closed with a negative answer

*Detail: `docs/status/M12b-P2.md`. §4 has the profile, §8 the A/B, §8.5 the
verdict.*

**The assignment:** profile the walk before touching it, then settle `bumpalo`
on data. M12 had measured the engine half at 54–85% of a render and deliberately
stopped, because that number covers three different things — allocation churn,
colour conversion, interpretation — and an arena answers only the first.

`perf` stayed blocked (`kernel.perf_event_paranoid` is 4, and lowering it needs
a root an agent must not take), so path (b) landed as PLAN.md specified: in-walk
phase timers and per-site allocation counters behind a default-off feature, kept
as permanent introspection. **The three-way split it produced is the item's
lasting output**, more than any change in it:

- colour conversion **≤ 7.2%**, and under 0.5% on three of four documents;
- allocation churn **0.6%** of the render after the no-dep pass (see §5 — P3
  later corrected the census this figure was taken on, without moving the
  verdict);
- interpretation **60–90% of the engine half**, and the majority on three of
  four documents.

**The no-dep path went first, as DEPS.md requires, and it took the traffic
away.** `RenderCaches` buffer reuse for the zero-area scan and the glyph
placement, plus an `Arc` in the glyph cache to stop copying an outline the
bitmap path never reads, cut the heaviest document from **3696 allocations per
render to 42** — worth −9.7% on `vector_paths_1751` and −6.0% / −5.5% / −4.3%
on the text documents, every one byte-identical on the scoreboard.

**Against that baseline the arena is +265% / +121% / +97% — slower**, at all
three object-count shapes the corpus presents, and slower than the untuned `Vec`
too. The cause is structural: a bump allocator is fast at handing out memory and
says nothing about touching it, and it cannot be reset per object because it is
borrowed by the vector it is filling — so it trades a buffer warm in L1 for a
fresh cold region every object. Independently disqualifying under PLAN.md's
rule: `RenderCaches` is `pub caches` on the facade's `RenderSession`, so `Bump`
would put a **lifetime in a public type**.

**`bumpalo` is NOT ADMITTED**, with a falsifiable reopening condition: a
workload where the walk's *own* allocation traffic exceeds a few percent of a
render after `RenderCaches` reuse. **DEPS.md has no open arena row.**

One footnote worth keeping (P2 §11): `bumpalo` may enter `Cargo.lock` anyway,
transitively through M12c's `wgpu` → `wasm-bindgen`. That does not reopen
anything. DEPS.md's tie-break toward in-tree crates applies to ties, and a free
supply chain does not make a slower implementation faster.

## 4. P3 — retargeted, and the milestone's largest result

*Detail: `docs/status/M12b-P3.md`. §5 has what landed, §6 the non-results, §7
the `pattern.rs` correction, §9 the ratchet.*

**P3 was rewritten mid-milestone, and that is the system working rather than a
plan failing.** As first written it aimed at `to_pixmap`'s per-pixel cost. P1
deleted the float round trip and the per-byte `/Decode` evaluation — the exact
loop-structure wins P3 was going to look for — and P2 measured what was left at
**1.2% of a warm engine half**, with `to_pixmap` totalling **0.1 ms** on
`image_en_fqa`, the document the original text leaned on. Two independent
measurements retired the premise before the item started, so it was re-aimed at
the one bucket P2's profile left standing: **interpretation**.

That is per-object *constant* overhead — 794 ns per object across 5010 objects
on `vector_paths_1751` — so the only lever is to do less per object. Ranked
before touching, then two changes landed, neither a loop nor an arena:

| change | measured |
|---|---|
| The cull test's exact box **bracketed** between a cheap subset and a cheap superset, solving a cubic only in between (`b7fe49e`) | **−14.8%** |
| The five heap buffers a path object took to hand the device one geometry, reduced to one (`d51ef60`) | **−13.3%** |

Together: engine half **2.526 → 1.451 ms (−42.6%)**, per-object constant
**504 → 290 ns**, whole render **−27.9%**. Both byte-identical on the
conformance scoreboard over all 1675 files. Criterion confirms it independently
at **−14.5% / −10.4% / −9.4%** on the exact, vello and tinyskia backends — and
**three unrelated rasterizers cannot agree on a 9–15% win unless the saving is
in the engine code they share**, which is the load-bearing fact of the whole
milestone.

**Two of the five named suspects were nothing**, and saying so is the point of
ranking before touching: the `RenderCtx` threading is one field-set per render
on a flat object list, and **no measurement found a cost in the dispatch
`match`** — a five-variant jump table was never the expense. What the arms
*call* was.

## 5. What P3 corrected in P2, and what it did not

P3 re-measured P2's territory before acting on it and found two things wrong.
Both are recorded here because a milestone document that only propagates its
items' conclusions cannot catch this class of error.

- **P2's "allocation in the walk is 0.6%" was a partial census.** The `Site`
  list covered `walk.rs` and `clip.rs`; with `paint.rs` and `path.rs` counted,
  the same render was making **21 421 allocations and 5.6 MiB**, not the
  handful the figure implied.
- **`pattern.rs` is exonerated.** P2 flagged `shading_axial_radial`'s engine
  half as "61% `pattern.rs` residue" and handed it on as unowned. It is not
  `pattern.rs`. It is the Coons/tensor mesh rasterizer in `shading/patch.rs`,
  which `Phase::Shading` never wrapped because the mesh kinds do not reach
  `draw_to_pixmap` — so it was in **no measurement bucket at all**.

**Neither correction reverses a P2 verdict.** The arena is still declined: the
A/B in P2 §8 measured the real renderer against a tuned baseline and does not
depend on the site census, and 21 421 allocations that P3's own change then
removed still bought what P3 measured, not what an arena would have. Colour
conversion stays closed at ≤ 7.2%. What moves is the *inheritance*:
`pattern.rs` needs no owner, and **`shading/patch.rs` does.** Read its numbers
carefully, because the headline overstates the target: the new `Phase::Patches`
bucket is **28.7 ms on `shading_axial_radial` and 47.1 ms on
`shading_tcpdf_030`**, but like `path prep` it *wraps the device calls it
makes*, so it contains its own RASTER. **Netting that out leaves roughly 2.5 ms
and 6.1 ms of engine-side patch work** — which is nonetheless essentially the
whole of each document's unattributed residue, and the largest such cost left in
the corpus. The shape of the question is a subdivision depth: 28 368 `fill_path`
calls for 16 patches is about 1800 quads per patch. `shading_coons` — a Coons
document with **zero** patch calls and a 0.02 ms engine half — is the control to
start from.

## 6. M12c — correct, isolated, and unusable by the consumer it was justified by

*Detail: `docs/status/M12c.md`. §1 has the version finding, §8 the benchmark,
§10 the scorecard.*

M12c ran in parallel with P1–P3 as an independent crate and is scored in its own
document; it is summarized here because the milestone's honest account needs it.

**What works.** `pdfrum-raster-vello-gpu` renders all 44 bench documents on an
RTX 4090 with **zero Tier-C interior differences** and no page where one backend
painted and the other did not. Isolation is `scripts/check-no-wgpu.sh`, run by
`ci.sh` and **verified by negative control** — one edge added from a leaf
backend was caught on four crates at once. `cargo-deny` needed no relaxation of
`cc`/`cmake`/`bindgen`. The facade's default is unchanged; it cannot name this
backend.

**What does not.** The exemption in DEPS.md is bought entirely with the claim
that pdfrum's likely consumer is a Rust GUI that already holds an open `wgpu`
device. Device injection is implemented and is the primary constructor — and
**vello 0.10 pins `wgpu` 29, while `egui-wgpu` 0.36 requires `^30.0` and
`iced_wgpu` 0.14 requires `^27.0`.** Two `wgpu` majors in one tree are unrelated
types, proved by compiling it. vello sits *between* two of the three frontends
PLAN.md names and matches neither. **The exemption's cost is paid here and its
benefit arrives on someone else's release schedule.** That is a finding about
the milestone's own premise, not a pin detail, and PLAN.md's original version
claim is withdrawn in place rather than deleted.

**The speed target is half met.** `vector` delivers **4.8x on the heaviest
vector page**; `shading` is **2.96x slower**, and the backend is 2.64x slower
than `vello_cpu` on rasterization overall once a null backend shows four of its
apparent wins are pure decode common to both columns. The missed shading target
and M12c.md §7's edge divergence are **one finding**: the engine decomposes patches into
thousands of tiny fills — the same decomposition that made SIMD useless in M12
§3.9 — which is the structure a GPU is worst at and the one this milestone
forbade restructuring. The crossover is not a threshold: page *shape* predicts
the winner, not size.

**The number future GPU work should start from** is not a shader figure:
`RasterBackend` returns a `Pixmap`, so every transparency group, soft mask and
pattern cell is a host round trip. The 2.64x is an **interface property**.

## 7. Measurement integrity: four near-false findings, and how each was caught

**This is the milestone's real character and it belongs in the record.** This
machine ran unrelated jobs at 300–455% CPU for the whole session — load average
between 6 and 20, never idle — and the milestone produced *four* findings that
were wrong, confident, and reproducible enough to publish. None was published.
The mechanisms differ, and a future reader who sees a surprising number on this
machine needs all four shapes:

**1. A regression on files the change cannot touch** (P1 §5.1, recalled at
§9.2). A first `cargo bench` of the `image` class reported **+5% to +25%** on
four documents this milestone's changes cannot reach — Flate, CCITT and JBIG2
files, none of which run `apply_codec_decode` at all. Load average 15, two
unrelated Python jobs at 300% CPU each. Re-run in a quieter window, every one
was back inside its band. *Mechanism: ambient load.* **Caught by the shape of
the result** — a change that cannot reach a file cannot slow it down — and the
numbers were discarded rather than reported. The replacement measurement was
**paired**: two binaries from two clean worktrees run *alternately* in one
window, so whatever the machine is doing hits both sides equally. That harness
is the source of every A/B in P1, P2 and P3.

**2. A 9–13% regression bisected to documentation** (P1 §9.3). `ratchet check`
put `vector_en_tem` slower by 9–13% on all three backends, which is the shape of
a real regression rather than noise, and the file does carry two `/DCTDecode`
images. Bisected across four commits, the readings came out **non-monotonic**:
109, 125, 107, 123 ms — and `85f47b7` differs from `bfb6f42` in **documentation
files only** (`diff -rq` over `crates/` reports one README), so a 15% gap
between those two is arithmetically impossible from code. Rebuilding each into a
fresh target directory reproduced each figure exactly, so it is not build
nondeterminism either: **stable per source tree and independent of the source
tree's contents.** *Mechanism: unknown, and stated as unknown.* The conclusion
recorded is that `vector_en_tem` is not measurable to better than ~15% on this
machine in its current state, and **no claim about it in either direction can be
supported** — which is a stronger statement than either "regression" or "noise".

**3. A contaminated bench, recognized on sight and thrown away** — the one whose
paper trail is thinnest, and it is worth saying so precisely. `+66.8%` on a
single entry, discarded before it entered any argument. *Mechanism: a competing
job starting mid-run.* **Caught by looking at the machine, not at the number** —
a delta that large on a document the change cannot plausibly have moved is a
contamination signature, not a result.

That one is recorded **only in `scripts/ratchet-decide.py`**, whose `R5`
threshold (`CONTAMINATION_PCT = 40.0`) carries it as the comment justifying the
constant, attributed there to P2. No status document records the figure, and P2
§12 instead records the same session's other contention symptom: three aborted
`cargo bench` runs, one of which managed **6 of 264 benchmarks in fifteen
minutes** at load 20. **The number survived into a committed threshold rather
than into prose**, which is a better fate than most discarded measurements get
but leaves this the least independently checkable of the four. It is listed with
that caveat rather than restated as though a status doc backed it.

**4. A +5% regression that survived bisection and did not exist** (P3 §4). The
worst of the four, because everything about it looked right. `pdfrum-bench`'s
`profile` binary has two render loops: the plain loop rebuilds the page graph
every iteration, and on a content-heavy document that is mostly `pdfrum-page`.
On `vector_en_tem` the plain loop reports **34 ms** and `--sample` **6.2 ms** —
the 28 ms difference is the rebuild, **82% of what the plain loop was timing.**
Measured there, P3's changes looked like a **+5.0% regression, reproducible
across twelve interleaved rounds**, median and minimum agreeing. Bisected,
*each half regressed it independently* (+6.4% and +13.5%) — which is already
incoherent, since two changes that each cost 6–13% do not compose to 5%.

The instrument settled it in one run: on that document the changed code is
**2.1% of an engine half that is 23% of the render**, about half a percent of
the whole, so a 5% regression there is arithmetically impossible. The
`--sample` phase table before and after agrees to three decimals on every row,
RASTER and ENGINE alike, and the same document re-measured is **−1.1%**.
*Mechanism: the harness measuring a different stage than the one under test.*
**Caught by an arithmetic upper bound on the possible effect** — the only one of
the four that a quieter machine would not have fixed.

**The general lesson, and it is not "the machine was loaded".** Three of the
four were caught by asking whether the claimed effect was *possible* — a
documentation-only diff cannot move code, a change worth 0.5% of a render cannot
cost 5%, a file the change does not touch cannot regress. Reproducibility was
worth nothing in cases 2 and 4: both reproduced perfectly, across bisections. On
this machine, **an A/B's consistency is not evidence; only its mechanism is.**

A fifth, related trap is documented at P3 §9.4 and is not a false finding but a
way to get one: `ratchet check` reads whatever `target/criterion/**` holds, with
**no indication of its age**. During this session that was a run from
2026-08-30, predating every commit in the milestone, reported as though it were
a verdict on today's tree. The stale directory was deleted so the tool now says
"run `cargo bench --workspace` first" rather than answering from history. Check
the mtime before trusting it.

## 8. The bench ratchet: the debt is unpaid, and the warm numbers say why that is not a verdict

`benches/baseline.json` is **NOT re-baselined by this milestone.** PLAN.md's
rule is that an entry which moves gets re-baselined in the same commit that
moves it, and entries moved a great deal. This is a **debt, not a decision**,
and it is stated as owed in §9.

**The full run was taken.** 264 of 266 benchmarks over six groups,
single-threaded, at load 6–9 with no contamination signature.
`scripts/ratchet-decide.py` — whose rules were **committed at benchmark 57 of
266, while the run was still in its first group** — returns **exit 2, leave the
update unwritten**.

**The verdict reproduces from this repository alone**, and it was re-verified
independently while writing this document:

```
python3 scripts/ratchet-decide.py docs/status/data/M12b-P3-bench.txt   # exit 2
```

### The warm family, which is the convention M12's exit target is judged on

**132 entries measured, 32 improved, ZERO regressed.** Not one entry above band
in the family that isolates the render.

| | baseline | measured | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `render-warm-exact/vector_paths_1751` | 10.444 ms | 8.934 ms | **−14.5%** |
| `render-warm-vello/vector_paths_1751` | 12.099 ms | 10.842 ms | **−10.4%** |
| `render-warm-tinyskia/vector_paths_1751` | 14.143 ms | 12.811 ms | **−9.4%** |
| `render-warm-exact/vector_font_size14` | 18.120 ms | 16.252 ms | −10.3% |
| `render-warm-exact/text_foxit_products` | 27.968 ms | 25.384 ms | −9.2% |
| *worst warm entry, inside band* | 132.549 ms | 136.780 ms | +3.2% |

Three independent rasterizer backends agreeing on a 9–15% win on the same
document is only possible if the saving is in the engine code they share.

### The cold family, which is substantially a `pdfrum-page` measurement

**132 entries, 61 improved, 8 regressed — every regression in the milestone is
in this family, none in warm.** `render-cold` builds a fresh `RenderSession`
inside the timed closure, so it re-derives the page graph every iteration; on
`vector_en_tem` that is ~94% page build and 6% render.

Three of the eight are the **pre-argued** `vector_en_tem` (§7's case 4), now
confirmed on all three backends (+7.6% / +5.5% / +4.2%) — itself corroboration,
because the effect tracks the *document* rather than the rasterizer, exactly as
a build-dominated benchmark should. The other five have **no pre-argued case,
and P3 declined to give them one**:

| entry | cold | **warm, same document + backend** |
|---|---:|---:|
| `shading_coons` / tinyskia | **+25.1%** | **−3.8%** |
| `shading_gouraud` / tinyskia | **+20.3%** | **−3.1%** |
| `forms_signature` / tinyskia | +5.2% | −1.1% |
| `forms_number` / tinyskia | +4.1% | −1.5% |
| `shading_type4_5` / exact | +3.2% | **−4.8%** |

Every one is regressed in exactly **one** of its four group/backend cells while
improving in the other three, including its own warm counterpart on the same
backend — and a code regression cannot be simultaneously backend-specific and
cold-specific while warm on that backend improves. On the two largest, a
self-A/B of **byte-identical binaries** differed by −4.4% and +7.0%; these are
0.28 ms renders where the noise floor exceeds the effect.

**The refusal is the point, and it is why the one exemption retains any value.**
`shading_type4_5` had five independent lines of evidence that its render is
unchanged (P3 §9.3), including a phase table showing zero `path prep` calls. It
was **not exempted**, because its case would have been built *after* it blocked,
and R1 forbids exactly that. Retrofitting a case for five entries standing
between an item and a green `update` would demonstrate that any inconvenient
entry can acquire an argument — which would make the pre-argued `vector_en_tem`
exemption worthless. **The exemption is worth something only because the other
five were refused.**

**A ratchet that declines to write a file is not a verdict on the engineering.**
The two facts are consistent: the ratchet declined to write, and the warm family
returned 132 entries with zero regressions and a 9–15% win on the target
document across three independent backends. The second is the one that answers
this milestone.

This one arrives from outside the four items and is recorded here so it is not
rediscovered. A font-substitution investigation (its own fix reverted under the
monotone rule; patch preserved at `scratchpad/croscore-fix/`) found that
`resources/pixel/bug_601362`'s residual is **not** a substitution error — with
the oracle's own chosen face force-loaded into both engines the pixel delta is
unchanged. What is missing is an unimplemented render feature:
`CPDF_Font::LoadCharPositions` (cpdf_font.cpp:449-467), gated by
`ShouldApplyGlyphSpacingHeuristic` (cpdf_font.cpp:240-264), which on
`pdf_glyph_width < font_glyph_width` sets `adjust_matrix_ = [pdf/font, 0, 0, 1]`
— a pure horizontal scale. On that file `/MissingWidth 506` against Arimo's `A`
advance of 667 gives 0.759, and at 40pt the oracle's glyph is 20px wide where
ours is 28px at identical height: exactly that factor.
`crates/pdfrum-render/src/text.rs` threads only `dest_width` into `GlyphKey`,
which reaches the MM design space alone. **`SubstFont::is_actual_font_loaded`
already exists and is dead code — it is precisely the gate this heuristic
needs.** Both branches (the origin shift and the horizontal scale) are
unimplemented. Two caveats: the evidence comes from a branch that was reverted,
so it should be re-derived before being built on; and the same investigation
noted a separate scoring divergence — our `SystemFontDb::describe` derives the
serif bit from PANOSE while `CFX_FolderFontInfo` sets it only when the face
*name* contains "Serif", so Tinos and Gelasio carry `STYLE_SERIF` for us and not
for the oracle. It did not bite this corpus but it is real in
`FaceInfo::similarity_score`.
